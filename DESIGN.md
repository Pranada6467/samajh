# Samajh — design v2 (working name)

> A post-sale understanding callback for Indian lenders: a teach-back-style comprehension check. The customer explains in their own words, in their own language, what they bought with their loan. Plain code compares that against the bank's records, and every mismatch becomes a flag a human can act on.

Status: **v2 design, 2026-11-10.** v2 applies the claims check in `references/claims/` (`design-corrections.md` for the edits, `claims-ledger.md` for the sources; ledger row ids such as A16 appear in brackets below). How we chose this idea: `IDEAS-HILLCLIMB.md` (5 rounds, final score 84/100). Amounts pilot: `references/pilots/amounts-pilot-2026-11-10.md`. Amounts and phone-line evaluation (240 TTS sentences, no human speech): `eval/amounts/REPORT.md`. Extractor and text-path evaluation (scripted text, no speech): `references/pilots/W5-extractor-report.md`. Call state machine and scripts (`samajh/dialogue/`, `data/prompts/`; tested on text, never voiced): `references/pilots/W9-dialogue-report.md`. Where the text says "our reading" or "our choice", the conclusion is ours and no regulator or court has ruled on it.

## 1. The problem

A gold-loan borrower complained that ₹52,000 had been deducted from his loan amount, and that the bank had described it as a "fixed deposit for loan security". It was a life-insurance policy. The Insurance Ombudsman found he had asked for cancellation within two months and ordered the insurer to cancel the policy and refund the premium (Bhopal, BHP-L-021-1617-0529).

Insurers already make verification calls, and in some awards the recorded call did not save the insurer:
- BNG-L-041-1617-0797: the insurer submitted the recording of the customer confirming the policy. The customer said the bank had assured him the money could be withdrawn after a year. The Ombudsman held the insurer responsible for its agent and ordered the policy converted.
- PUN-L-036-1617-0228: "The PIVC recording is clear about the terms and conditions of the policy but the maturity benefit is not explained." Refund ordered.
- PUN-L-017-1617-0684: the recording showed the caller's "main focus ... was on processing the application". Refund ordered.

In four awards the complainant said they had been told how to answer the call. One was told "there would be a verification call and in reply she had to answer everything in positive" (AHD-L-001-1617-0203). In another the Ombudsman accepted that the customer "responded as was guided" (CHD-L-019-1718-1436).

A recorded "yes" is weak evidence of understanding. It is not worthless. Insurers have won where the recording showed the caller stating the disputed point and the customer agreeing (PUN-L-013-1617-0583), and where the Ombudsman heard the recording and the customer had confirmed the proposal details (NOI-L-036-1819-0337). Both of those complaints were also made late.

## 2. The rule that makes this timely

On 15 June 2026 RBI issued ten entity-specific Amendment Directions on "Advertising, Marketing and Sale of Financial Products and Services by Regulated Entities". They take effect on **1 January 2027**. Commercial banks: Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks - Responsible Business Conduct) Second Amendment Directions, 2026, RBI/2026-27/115, para 85Y. NBFCs: RBI/2026-27/123, para 101Y. Small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, urban and rural co-operative banks and All India Financial Institutions have the same paragraph under their own numbers. Housing finance companies follow NBFC paragraphs 101A to 101ZA through para 157A of the HFC Directions (RBI/2026-27/124). RBI's consolidated Directions pages did not yet show paragraphs 85A to 85ZA on 10 November 2026, so we cite the amendment.

> "85Y. A bank shall establish a mechanism to seek feedback from customers, within a period of 30 days from the sale of any financial product / service to ensure that customers have understood the features of the financial product / service and also the risks associated with such financial product / service. The mechanism may include selection of the customers on a random basis for such feedback through modes such as call-backs or surveys carried out by a department / vertical of the bank not associated with sale of products / services. A half-yearly report on the findings of the feedback shall be prepared and utilised for review of existing policies and features of financial products / services."

Samajh is a tool a bank's non-sales unit can use to run that mechanism.

85Y requires four things: a feedback mechanism, feedback sought within 30 days of the sale, the purpose of ensuring customers understood features and risks, and a half-yearly report used to review policies and products. Random selection, call-backs, surveys and the independent unit sit in a sentence that begins "may include". 85Y does not say who receives the report and requires no filing with RBI.

We add checks against the related paragraphs of the same chapter to the 85Y callback. 85Y doesn't require these checks, but the bank must meet those paragraphs anyway, and the evidence falls out of the same call:

| Para (commercial banks) | What it says | What we check |
|---|---|---|
| 85G | Explicit consent for each product (for example a signed declaration, OTP, digitally recorded confirmation, or a demarcated clause), kept until one year after the contract ends | Is there a consent record for each product, and does the customer recognise it? |
| 85V | No compulsory bundling of a third-party product with the bank's own. A product required as a risk mitigant is allowed if the customer may buy it from any provider | Was the product required for the loan? If so, is it a risk mitigant, and was the customer offered a choice of provider? |
| 85W | No paying for a product out of the loan without explicit consent | Was the premium deducted from the loan, and did the customer know? |
| 4(20A)(ii) | Mis-selling includes incorrect, incomplete or misleading information | Does what the customer believes contradict the product's features? A wrong belief is evidence that incorrect information may have been given, not proof |
| 85Z | Where mis-selling is established, the bank refunds the full amount paid and compensates loss under its own policy. A customer may also complain within a set window | We prepare an evidence pack for the bank's reviewer |

For NBFCs the sub-paragraph letters are the same and the prefix changes: 101G, 101V, 101W, 101Y, 101Z. The mis-selling definition is para 6(9A) for NBFCs, not 4(20A) (A5, A19).

RBI does not define "risk mitigant". Treating credit-life cover on a loan as one is our reading. A savings policy described as a "security deposit" or an "FD" is not a risk mitigant on any reading (A16).

## 3. Users

- **Primary:** the department of a bank or NBFC that doesn't sell products and runs the 85Y feedback mechanism (customer experience, compliance or audit).
- **Secondary:** the compliance head who owns the half-yearly report; voice-agent vendors (Gnani included) who sell to these lenders.

Insurance sold with a loan is a "third-party product or service" that the bank sells under an agency or referral arrangement with the provider, so the bank owns the duties of this chapter for that sale (A24).

## 4. v1 scope

**In v1**
- One product family: a loan (personal or gold) sold with credit-life / loan-protection insurance.
- Languages: Hindi, Hinglish, Tamil and Kannada. A language counts as **supported** only with a fluent reviewer for its scripts and scenarios, plus ≥10 improvised real-voice calls. That bar is the target. **Today no human voice recordings exist, so every language is "TTS-only, not human-tested", and the Tamil and Kannada text is "not human-reviewed".** Regional languages are our choice: 85R covers documents for the bank's own products and defers to the product regulator for third-party products, so it does not ground the language choice for insurance (A25).
- One outbound call flow, made from a simulated phone in the browser.
- Checks: 85Y understanding, 85G, 85V, 85W, and belief mismatch (which may engage 4(20A)(ii)).
- Outputs: a flagged case with evidence, an evidence pack for review, and a half-yearly summary view.
- The headline experiment: teach-back-style open questions vs yes/no on the same scenarios.

**Not in v1**
- Real telephony and real bank integrations (all records and calls are synthetic). The number series and telecom declarations in §5 describe a real deployment; the demo does not exercise them.
- Mutual funds, credit-card add-ons and other products (they're headroom, and the same engine).
- Analysing the original sales call.
- Deciding whether something *is* mis-selling: we only flag cases for human review.
- Testing whether the product was suitable or appropriate for the customer (4(20A)(i)).
- Re-teaching: Samajh assesses understanding and does not explain the product.
- Live streaming STT (we use REST per turn with a browser end-of-speech detector at 700 ms; decision 12 in §15, closed by measurement).

## 5. The call

```
right-party check → disclosure → second check → context → open recall
  → teach-back-style questions per product → consent check → read-back → close
```

1. **Right-party check.** "Am I speaking with <first name>?" If not, the bot ends the call and says nothing about a loan. An unclear reply is asked once more (without the lender's name); a second unclear reply ends the call (`not_confirmed`).
2. **Disclosure.** The bank's name and "customer-care team". This is not a sales call and nothing will be offered. The caller is an automated AI assistant. The call is for feedback, it is recorded to keep an accurate record of that feedback, and the bank's written notice explains how the data is used. The customer can decline, stop at any time, or ask for a human. The customer can hang up and call the customer-care number printed on the papers from the lender to check the call is genuine (the loan is not named until step 4). The bot will never ask for an OTP, PIN, password or account number. It then asks "Shall we go ahead?": a no ends the call as `declined`, and an unclear reply is asked once more and then ends it as `no_go_ahead`.
3. **Second check, no secret.** One low-sensitivity fact, the year of birth (synthetic in the demo). The bot never asks for account digits. The customer gets two tries, and a mismatch and an unclear reply get the same re-ask line, so the bot never confirms a wrong guess as wrong. A two-digit year ("84") passes only when the last two digits of the year on record are 10 or more, so "one" or "two" in "say that again" cannot pass for 2001 or 2002. A speaker who says they are someone else ("I am his wife, he was born in 1984") fails the check even with the right year. Failure ends the call with no mention of the loan (`second_check_failed`, or `wrong_party` for a speaker who says they are someone else).
4. **Context.** Only now does the bot mention the loan.
5. **Open recall (the key question).** *"Loan ke saath aapne kya-kya liya, aur kitne ka?"* This is asked before we mention any product, so we don't lead the answer.
6. **Teach-back-style questions for each product on record.** The bot never names the product's kind, so none of the spoken lines holds the words insurance, policy, premium, cover, FD or deposit (the lint in `samajh/dialogue/lint.py` rejects them). With one product on record it says "the thing taken with the loan"; with several it says "the first / second thing on our record with the loan". The nine questions are asked in this order, in the customer's language, and as spoken they are: "What do you think that thing is?"; "How much did you pay for it?"; "Where and how was that money paid?" (open, not "from the loan amount, or separately?"); "Was it needed to get the loan, or was it your choice? What were you told?"; "What were you told about where to take it from?"; "What do you get from it, and up to what amount?"; "How long is it for?"; "Were you told you could cancel it, and until when?"; "If you wanted this money back, what would happen?" The list is the same for every record: the provider-choice question is always asked, though the rules need the answer only when the product was required, because which questions are asked would otherwise reveal `required_for_loan`. The bot compares the cancel answer with `cancel_window_days` on the record and never states a right itself. The money-back question is open and is asked for every product. What the customer believes about getting the money back is compared with the product's features (`belief_mismatch`, §6). It is how a belief such as "the money can be withdrawn after a year" (BNG-L-041-1617-0797) comes out.
7. **Consent check.** "When you took the loan, what did you say yes to, and how did you say yes?" (as spoken; the lint bans "buy" and any suggested method such as "sign" or "form"). The bot compares the answer with the consent record. The call never *captures* consent.
8. **Read-back.** The premium the customer stated is read back, in their language, with the figure the customer said and never the record's ("आपने बताया था बावन हज़ार, यानी 52,000। क्या यह सही है?", that is, "You said fifty-two thousand, that is 52,000. Is that right?"). Only the premium is read back; the cover, term and cancel window are asked once and not read back. A yes closes the item. A reply that names another figure ("not fifty-two, fifty-five thousand") gets "ठीक है। तो सही रक़म क्या है?" ("So what is the right amount?"), and a figure the parser reads cleanly is read back in its turn. "I don't know" is recorded as unresolved and not pressed ("कोई बात नहीं, आगे बढ़ते हैं"). There are at most three read-backs per product; after that the item is recorded as unresolved. The amounts evaluation showed why (§6; TTS audio only, no human speech): on simulated phone audio, Prisma v3.0 gave a wrong amount that the parser could not detect on 17 of 240 sentences. One pilot example (n = 1, TTS voice, simulated 8 kHz phone audio): Prisma v3.0 returned 2,00,000 for "paune do lakh".
9. **Close.** "Thank you." The close adds "our team may call you again" only when the live call left something unresolved (an unclear answer, a rejected read-back, the turn budget). The call cannot say more: flags are computed after the call by the extractor and the rules, so the bot has no flag to announce and promises no call "within N days". Whether and how the bank follows up a flag is outside the dialogue (open). The close mentions no other product.

Unclear answers are asked once more in simpler words, then recorded as unresolved (`EndOfCall.unresolved` in code, which the design earlier called `unclear → human`). "I don't know" is an answer and is not re-asked. One re-ask and no more also avoids the "nagging" pattern in RBI's dark-pattern annex (A20). The same reason limits the question "Do you want to stop this call?": see "The call as implemented" below. The bot never argues with the customer and never explains the product during the call. That would contaminate the test, and explaining belongs to a human follow-up. Where the bot names the insurer it uses the name on the record and adds nothing, because 85J bars a bank from presenting a third-party product as its own (A24). The call carries no sales content of any kind.

**Why the opening changed from design v1.** Design v1 asked for the last 4 digits of the loan account and the date of birth, and named the loan before confirming who had answered. RBI's public advice says: "Never share your account details such as account number, login ID, password, PIN, UPI-PIN, OTP, ATM / Debit card / credit card details with anyone, not even with bank officials, however genuine they might sound." That is public-awareness text, not a direction to lenders, and it does not name loan-account digits or a date of birth. An unknown caller asking for them still looks like the pattern it describes, and a customer who refuses is behaving sensibly (E5.2, E5.3). Naming the loan first would disclose it to whoever answers. Not doing so is good practice by analogy with the privacy rules RBI sets for other kinds of call (85N(6), the 2026 recovery amendment). We found no RBI text that prescribes how to authenticate a customer on an outbound call (E5.1). A first-name "yes" alone is a weak check, which is why step 3 exists.

**What grounds the opening.**
- The call is made from the lender's 1600-series number. Required for service and transactional calls: TRAI direction of 19 November 2025 para 8(iii); RBI/2024-25/105. No TRAI text classifies a feedback call. We treat it as a service call.
- The call carries no promotional content. Under the TCCCPR, promotional content makes it a promotional call, which needs the 140 series and consent.
- The lender tells its telecom provider in advance that it uses automated calls. Required now: TCCCPR regulation 4 as substituted in February 2025. The Third Amendment of 18 September 2026 widens this to a declaration of all application-to-person calls, and treats undeclared calls as unsolicited commercial communication, from 60 days after gazette publication.
- The AI line and the offer of a human. Recommended by RBI's FREE-AI committee report (para 4.4.54, read in a mirror copy). We found no law that requires them.
- The recording line. Our choice. The only RBI recording duty we found is for recovery calls.
- The purpose, the right to decline, and a pointer to a written notice. Required by the DPDP Act sections 5 and 6 from 13 May 2027 where the lender relies on consent.
- Calls are placed between 10:00 and 19:00. Our choice. We found no RBI or TRAI hour limit that applies in terms to a feedback call. 85N(4) sets 09:00 to 19:00 for sales staff and the card rules set 10:00 to 19:00.

### The call as implemented

Built 2026-11-10 as a pure core, `samajh/dialogue/`, with the bot's words in `data/prompts/` (51 lines per language, each naming the paragraph above that it implements). Evidence and review: `references/pilots/W9-dialogue-report.md`. The code wins if this text differs. It takes text and returns text: no audio, and no network except the optional LLM fallback. **It is not connected to the phone yet**: the server still runs its three-question `StubDialogue`, and the adapter that fetches the second Prisma reading on amount turns is iteration 2 work.

```
start(record, language, lender_name=...) -> (CallState, BotUtterance)
step(state, customer_text, second_reading=None, interpreter=...) -> (CallState, BotUtterance | EndOfCall)
```

`CallState.position` says which reply the bot waits for:

| Position | The bot waits for | A good reply leads to | Ends the call as |
|---|---|---|---|
| `AtIdentity` | yes to the first name | go-ahead question | `wrong_party` (a no, or a speaker who says they are someone else); `not_confirmed` (unclear twice) |
| `AtGoAhead` | yes to "shall we go ahead?" | second check | `declined` (a no); `wrong_party`; `no_go_ahead` (unclear twice) |
| `AtBirthYear` | the year of birth | context line, then open recall: the first time the loan is named | `wrong_party`; `second_check_failed` (two tries) |
| `AtRecall` | the open recall answer | first teach-back question | |
| `AtTeachBack(product, question)` | one of the nine answers | next question, next product, then consent | |
| `AtConsent` | the consent answer | the first read-back, or the close when no premium was stated | |
| `AtReadBack`, `AtRestate` | yes, no or a figure | the next read-back, then the close | |
| `AtStopCheck` | yes or no to "do you want to stop this call?" | back to the pending question | `declined`; `human_requested` |

All eight outcomes: `completed`, `wrong_party`, `not_confirmed`, `no_go_ahead`, `second_check_failed`, `declined`, `human_requested`, `turn_limit`. After a refusal or a request for a human the bot says one closing line and stops; it gives no reason to stay. The wrong-party, not-confirmed, no-go-ahead and failed-check endings say nothing about a loan.

**Limits, so that a call always ends.** One re-ask per question. At most 60 bot utterances (`Limits.max_bot_turns`), 3 read-backs per product (`max_read_backs`) and 2 stop questions per call (`max_stop_checks`); a third stop signal ends the call. Tested on text: 200 random customers per language, each against two records, ended inside their own bounds with the budget raised to 10,000, and 1,200 calls driven by an interpreter returning random intents all ended. A three-product call in which every answer is unclear can hit the 60-utterance budget before all questions are asked.

**The call keeps no tested value.** `start` keeps a plan (loan id, first name, birth year, product ids) and nothing else from the record. No line can depend on the kind, premium, payment source, cover, term, cancel window, consent or provider choice, and the only figures the bot says are ones the customer said first. Test: two records that differ in every tested value got word-for-word identical bot lines over 200 random customers in each of four languages, and over 1,200 random-interpreter calls; no line held a record value. The bot's words do show that a product is on record and how many ("the first thing"); the design asks per product, so this stays.

**A third party is not told about the loan.** Phrases that say the speaker is not the borrower or speaks of the borrower as someone else ("I am his wife", "he is at work, tell me", "wrong number, 1984") are read at the first three steps, before the loan is named, and end the call as `wrong_party`; the second check looks for them before it looks for the year. Before the review fix, 9 of 21 identity probes in which the reply was about the borrower in the third person counted as a yes, and a wife's "he was born in 1984" passed the second check. Still open: see the risks in §13.

**The read-back uses the customer's own figure, and a correction is handled.** With 61,000 on the record and "fifty-two thousand" said, the bot reads back 52,000. If the customer says no, or names another figure in a sentence the parser cannot settle, the bot asks for the right amount and never speaks a figure from the unsettled reply. A figure the parser reads cleanly is read back in its turn, up to the limit.

**An optional second Prisma reading.** `step(..., second_reading=text)` takes the transcript of the same audio from the other Prisma model on a turn that asks for a figure (§15 item 16; elsewhere it is ignored). `samajh.amounts.cross_check` says whether the two readings agree. Every stated premium is read back whatever the readings say; where they differ, the call reads back the first reading unless only the second brings a figure the customer has not just heard or rejected, so a disagreement forces a spoken read-back of a figure the customer must confirm, and, because every stated premium is read back anyway, adds none; what it changes is which figure is read. A plain yes to a read-back stands. `Turn.text` holds the reading the call acted on, so the rule engine reads the same figure the bot read back; the other reading is kept in `CallState.second_readings` with an `agreed` flag, for the evidence log. Test: with the documented v3.0 mis-format (3,02,000 for "three lakh two hundred") and the true words as the two readings, the call reads back 3,02,000, the customer says no and restates, and the call then reads back 3,00,200 and the rule engine passes that premium. The pairs in the tests are hand-written: no real pair of Prisma transcripts has been played in.

**Reading the customer's turn.** Word lists in four languages (the rule interpreter) read each reply. An optional LLM fallback, off by default (`--llm`), is asked only about a reply the rules left as plain "said". It returns a label and nothing else: never a number or a year, and no label ends a call (its "decline" and "wants a human" lead to the stop question, and it cannot say "wrong person"). A failed LLM call is read as unclear and logged with the error type and model, never the customer's words. It is asked about 115 of the 408 test replies and adds a median 0.74 s of silence per call (p95 1.87 s over the first worker's 506 calls; median 0.77 s, p95 1.22 s over 42 uncached calls in the review rerun); whether live calls use it is open. A plain refusal ends the call at once. A weak signal gets the one stop question and is never argued with: a short "leave it", an LLM's guess, and (from open recall on, Hindi and English only) a refusal inside a sentence that recounts the sale ("I told them I am not interested but they said it was compulsory"), because those are the sentences the product exists to hear. A yes ends the call; anything else returns to the pending question as if its last reply had been unclear.

**Measured, and what limits it.** 408 customer replies (17 behaviours, 4 languages, 6 each) were written by `google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite` from a behaviour description and played into the real machine; the label is the effect the writer was told to produce. On fold 1 (n = 199, not used to tune the word lists, with one stated exception): rules alone gave the labelled effect on 155 (78%), rules plus the LLM fallback on 187 (94%); no call ended on a reply that was not a refusal. Fold 0 (n = 209, tuned on): 191 and 202. Per cell n is 3 to 9. Replies are synthetic text, not speech and not STT output; one writer model; some labels are wrong.

**Limits that apply to every dialogue result in this document.**
- The replies are synthetic text, not speech, and no human wrote or labelled them. The review's probe sentences are one reviewer's, also not from a caller; counts of probes are not rates.
- No fluent human has read any language's lines. Hindi and Hinglish were written by one agent and revised for register by another. Tamil and Kannada lines, word lists and scripted customers are not human-reviewed.
- The 671 tests (`tests/dialogue`) prove the code does what its authors specified; they do not show that a customer understands a line. Nothing has been voiced: whether Timbre reads the mixed-script Hinglish lines, "ए-आई" and "ओ-टी-पी" well is unchecked.
- The review's 11 fixes (W9b, who wrote none of the original code) have had no second independent review.

## 6. How it works

```
LOAN RECORD (synthetic)  ──►  DIALOGUE (samajh/dialogue + data/prompts/: line templates,
                              no value under test is kept)  ──►  bot lines (Timbre, 8 kHz; fixed lines
                              pre-generated, read-backs synthesised on the turn because they carry
                              the customer's figure)
                                                        │
                       customer audio (each turn) ◄─────┘
                                │
                     PRISMA REST (v3.0 transcribe; on amount turns v2.5 transcribe too)
                                │
                     AMOUNT NORMALISER (fraction-aware; two readings compared by `cross_check` inside the dialogue's `step`, see Amounts below)
                                │
                     EXTRACTOR (LLM via OpenRouter → strict schema, with a quoted span for each field)
                                │
                     RULE ENGINE (pure functions: answer vs record → flags + paragraph)
                                │
              ┌─────────────────┴──────────────────┐
        PRISMA BATCH on the full call          CASE + EVIDENCE LOG
        (segment timestamps, speakers)  ───►   (quotes with timestamps, paragraph, verdict)
                                                     │
                        EVIDENCE PACK (for the bank's 85Z review) · HALF-YEARLY VIEW (85Y)
```

**Core types (sketch)**
```python
# The lender's record (all frozen; unknown fields rejected)
Product    = {id, kind: "credit_life", provider, premium_inr, cover_inr, term_months,
              paid_from_loan: bool, required_for_loan: bool, risk_mitigant: bool,
              provider_choice_offered: bool, cancel_window_days: int | None,
              features: set of is_deposit|refundable_anytime|guaranteed_return|
                               one_time_payment|withdrawable_after_period,   # claims true of it
              consent: {method: signed_form|otp|digital_click|recorded_call|verbal, at: date} | None}
LoanRecord = {loan_id, lender_type: "bank"|"nbfc", borrower (synthetic),
              loan_kind: "personal"|"gold", sanctioned_inr, disbursed_inr, disbursed_on: date,
              products: [Product]}

# What the customer said: every answer is exactly one of four states
Answer[T]  = NotAsked | Unclear(reason) | DontKnow(quote, turn_id) | Stated(value, quote, turn_id)

# Two layers: the LLM only fills the first; deterministic code builds the second
RawExtraction  # enums and verbatim text; amounts and durations stay as the customer's words
  -> Beliefs   # rupees, months and days parsed; every quote found in the right customer turn

Flag       = {rule_id, clause, product_id (None if case-level), severity: high|medium|low,
              expected, heard, quote, turn_id}     # clause = CLAUSES[lender_type][rule_id]
Gap        = {rule_id, clause, product_id, reason, quote, turn_id}   # a rule could not decide
Verdict    = "clear" | "flag_for_review" | "unclear_needs_human"   # per product and per case
rule_id    = kind | premium | cover | term | cancel_window | recall | recall_unmatched |
             consent | loan_funded | bundling | belief_mismatch | sale_conduct | evidence

CLAUSES = {   # the paragraph a rule may engage, by lender type (A5, A19)
  "bank": {kind|premium|cover|term|cancel_window|recall|recall_unmatched: "85Y",
           consent: "85G",  bundling: "85V",  loan_funded: "85W",
           belief_mismatch: "4(20A)(ii)",  sale_conduct|evidence: "internal"},
  "nbfc": {kind|premium|cover|term|cancel_window|recall|recall_unmatched: "101Y",
           consent: "101G", bundling: "101V", loan_funded: "101W",
           belief_mismatch: "6(9A)(ii)",   sale_conduct|evidence: "internal"},
}
```
The sketch matches `samajh/types.py`; the code wins if they differ. Rule ids are neutral and the paragraph is looked up by lender type. `internal` marks a check of our own that engages no paragraph. Housing finance companies follow the NBFC paragraphs through para 157A. Other lender types change only the numeric prefix and are not mapped in v1.

**Rules.** These are deterministic and never call the LLM. The paragraph in brackets is the commercial-bank one:
- `kind` (85Y): the customer's stated kind ≠ the record (e.g. "FD" vs credit_life) → flag. A customer who says they do not know what the product is → flag (coordinator decision, §15 item 18). No usable answer → `unclear`.
- `premium` (85Y): |heard − record| > 1% (after normalising) → flag. A customer who says they do not know, or do not remember, the premium → flag (medium) (coordinator decision, §15 item 17). Only a question not asked, silence or an unusable answer → `unclear`. The premium **passes only after the customer has confirmed a read-back** of the amount they gave. An amount that matches the record but was never confirmed is `unclear`, and so is one whose read-back the customer rejected.
- `cover` / `term` (85Y): unaware or wrong → flag (lower severity).
- `cancel_window` (85Y): the customer says whether they were told they can cancel, and by when. The "by when" is read as days and compared with `cancel_window_days` on the record (1 day of tolerance). Not told, unsure whether they can cancel, a different "by when", or knowing they can cancel but not by when → flag (low). A missing "by when" (not asked, or no usable answer) → `unclear`. If the record shows no window and the customer says they were told they can cancel → flag (low). The code never states a right.
- `recall` (85Y): asked what they took with the loan, the customer does not name a product that is on the record → flag (low severity). Open recall not asked → `unclear`.
- `recall_unmatched` (85Y, case-level): the customer names an insurance, deposit or investment that is not on the record → flag (medium). A fee is ignored. Anything the extraction could not classify → `unclear`.
- `consent` (85G): `consent is None` → flag (the record alone is enough; the call adds the customer's words). A record on file passes only when the customer's own account, given to the open consent question, includes this product **and** names the same method of agreeing as `consent.method` on the record. A product the customer does not recognise → flag (high). A customer who says they do not know what they agreed to buy → flag (medium). A different method → flag (medium). A customer who agrees they bought it but cannot say how they agreed (or gives no usable answer about the method) → `unclear`. A record on file does not clear 85G by itself.
- `loan_funded` (85W): `paid_from_loan` and (no consent or the customer says it was paid separately / didn't know) → flag. If the record says the premium was paid separately and the customer does not know how it was paid → `unclear` (§15 item 19).
- `bundling` (85V): the product was required for the loan (per the record or the customer) **and** at least one of these holds: the record says it is not a risk mitigant; the customer describes it as a deposit (not a risk mitigant on any reading, A16); the record shows no choice of provider was offered; the customer says they were not told they could buy it elsewhere. Each reason is its own flag (high). Required as a risk mitigant with a choice of provider that the customer confirms, or not required per the record and the customer → no flag. A question not asked or not usably answered → `unclear`. Products with no premium (free of cost) are not modelled in v1: `premium_inr` must be above 0.
- `belief_mismatch` (may engage 4(20A)(ii)): belief contradicts the product's `features` (refundable / guaranteed return / FD / a single payment, when the record does not list `one_time_payment`; §15 item 20) → flag. The money-back question (§5 step 6) must be asked: without a usable answer the product can be at best `unclear`, and "I don't know" is a flag (low). The paragraph is the definition of mis-selling, so it is shown as the paragraph the flag may engage and never as a verdict.
- `sale_conduct` (internal, case-level): the customer voices a concern about how the sale or the confirmation was handled (told what to answer, learned of the product only after the sale, signed without an explanation) → flag (high).
- `evidence` (internal): a quote that is not in the call transcript supports no verdict → `unclear`.
- Any product with a flag → `flag_for_review`. Silence is never a pass: a question never asked or not usably answered is a gap, and a product with a gap and no flag is `unclear_needs_human`. A flag on `kind`, `premium`, `consent`, `bundling` or `loan_funded` also produces an evidence pack: the record, the customer's words with their turn ids, and the paragraph engaged. The bot never says "mis-selling".

**Why an evidence pack and not a refund case.** The 85Z refund duty arises "in cases where mis-selling of a financial product / service is established". The text does not say who establishes it or whether a complaint is needed, sets no refund deadline, and leaves compensation to the bank's "approved policy". A flag is not established mis-selling (A18).

**Amounts.** We use `format=transcribe`, then a fraction-aware parser (sawa / saadhe / paune / dedh / dhai; Tamil and Kannada 1.5 and 2.5). This replaces the 20-sentence pilot as our evidence.

*What was measured* (`eval/amounts/REPORT.md`, run 2026-11-10). **TTS audio only: no human speech was used**, so every figure is an upper bound for real callers and says nothing about them. The set is `data/amounts/amounts-v1.jsonl`, 240 sentences (60 each in Hindi, Hinglish, Tamil and Kannada; Tamil and Kannada not human-reviewed), sha256 `62b0dd71…5e0d76`, frozen before any audio. One Timbre voice per sentence, one amount per sentence, simulated phone audio (8 kHz mu-law), `format=transcribe` with the language set. Score: exact match on the rupee amount against the amount the generator used. Intervals are Wilson 95%.

- **Parser against naive digit reading, phone audio, n = 240.** v3.0: parser 221 (92%, 88-95), naive 220 (92%, 87-95). v2.5: parser 214 (89%, 85-92), naive 156 (65%, 59-71). On v2.5 the parser recovers 58 sentences, 43 of them fractions. In these cells the naive reading was never right where the parser was wrong. The 7-sentence gap between the two models is not established (McNemar p = 0.32).
- **v3.0 fails with confident wrong amounts.** 17 of its 19 phone failures are a wrong amount with no warning, 2 are no answer. 8 of the 19 are proven formatting errors by Prisma (its own `verbatim` output for the same sentence has the right words), 9 more have the same shape without a word output to prove it, and the parser can fix 1. Amounts that skip a group are the clearest case ("three lakh two hundred" comes back as 3,02,000): v3.0 reads 6 of 16 (38%, 18-61) against 161 of 164 other non-fraction amounts (98%, 95-99); v2.5 reads 15 of the 16. No parser can fix these from that transcript.
- **v2.5 fails with no answer.** 20 of its 26 phone failures are no answer. By the report author's labels (one reader, Tamil and Kannada without fluency), 22 of the 26 are partly formatted output that the parser does not yet compose.
- **Two readings that agree are almost always right.** On phone audio, v2.5 and v3.0 gave the same amount for 200 of 240 sentences and 199 of those 200 were right (the one miss is a sentence whose written form is suspect). In the other 40, where they differed or one gave no answer, at least one was right 37 times. Prisma returns no confidence score; agreement is a measured stand-in, at this n and on TTS audio only.
- **Noise.** The phone channel cost nothing measurable: mu-law 8 kHz against clean 16 kHz gave 214 against 216 of 240 on v2.5 and 221 against 221 on v3.0. The band-limit, street noise at 10 and 5 dB, and babble at 10 dB each stayed within 4 sentences of phone audio on the same sentences (112 to 128 sentences per cell). Only babble at 5 dB hurt: v3.0 fell from 102 to 93 of 112 (12 lost, 3 gained, p = 0.035, before any correction for the ten comparisons in that table) and v2.5 from 102 to 97 (p = 0.27). The noise is synthetic.

*Design consequence, adopted (coordinator decision, 2026-11-10; §15 item 16).* Each amount-bearing customer turn is transcribed by both Prisma v2.5 and v3.0, and `samajh.amounts.cross_check(first, second)` compares the two parsed readings (it returns `Agreed(rupees)` or `ReadBackNeeded(readings)`). If the two readings give the same amount, that is the amount. If they differ, or either gives no reading, the bot always reads the amount back and the customer confirms. The read-back stays for every critical amount regardless of agreement. v3.0 alone is not enough: its wrong amounts come with no warning.

**Extraction model: `openai/gpt-6-luna` through OpenRouter.** This is W5b's recommendation (`references/pilots/W5-extractor-report.md` §2.4). It replaces the earlier default, the cheap `mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct`, which `references/pilots/W2-clients-report.md` chose on a 16-utterance probe. The model returns enums, clause numbers and spans, never turn ids, product ids, rupee figures or verdicts. Code resolves them against the customer's reply, so every quote kept is a slice of the reply (frozen-set run: 0 of 802 spans not in the reply, 0 of 1,195 clause numbers out of range). The model rejects the `temperature` parameter (OpenRouter answers 404), so it is not pinned to 0 and a second uncached run could differ. `samajh.pipeline.default_llm()` leaves the parameter out. It is a reasoning model and needs room: at 800 output tokens one hard reply came back cut off and empty, so the limit is 4,000.

- **Cost.** $0.00171 per transcript on the frozen set (144 transcripts, 3,391 LLM requests, $0.24589 with nothing cached, $0.00007 per LLM request) and $0.00153 per transcript on the development set (n = 20 transcripts). That is about twice Mistral ($0.00081 on the development set) and a third of `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` ($0.00502).
- **Latency.** Per LLM request, when first made: median 1.88 s, p90 3.38 s (frozen-set run; wall time of the client call, retries included). A transcript takes 20 to 24 requests made one after another, about 40 s at the median. The 40 s is inferred from the per-request median, not measured end to end. **Consequence: extraction runs after the call, on the whole transcript, and not per turn.** It is fine after a call and too slow inside one. Anything the bot must decide during the call (the one re-ask of an unclear answer, §5; the read-back) cannot wait for the extractor. The dialogue decides these turns with word lists, plus the optional LLM fallback (§5, "The call as implemented"; measured on synthetic text only, in `references/pilots/W9-dialogue-report.md` §4). Running one transcript's requests in parallel is the option if the time ever matters (not built).
- **Why the earlier default was rejected.** On the development set (20 hand-written calls, 5 per language, 7 of them gold-clear), Mistral raised false alarms on 3 of the 7 gold-clear calls (`google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite`: 7 of 7; `openai/gpt-6-luna` and `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`: 0 of 7). Call verdicts right: gpt-6-luna 20 of 20, Haiku 20, Mistral 17, Gemini 13. Fields right (n = 304): gpt-6-luna 304, Haiku 303, Mistral 298, Gemini 285. The weaker models read a plain "I signed" or "I gave the OTP" as a sale concern. They also misread negations. On a probe of 24 Hindi and Hinglish replies written so that the first clause, read alone, gives the opposite answer, gpt-6-luna and Haiku read 24 of 24 correctly, Mistral 20 and Gemini 18. Two of Mistral's four misses were in the false-clear direction: a reply that begins "I agreed" and then denies it was read as consent given. The probe shows a failure pattern and is not a rate, and it has no Tamil or Kannada. A difference of one or two on these sets is noise (Mistral gave 18 of 20 verdicts in one run and 17 in another with the same prompts); the gap between the two groups of models is not. The development set is in-sample for the prompt, which was iterated on it, and the model was not selected on the frozen set (it is kept for one final measurement).
- **Alternative.** `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` matches gpt-6-luna on the probe, is one field behind on the development set, costs 3.3 times as much per transcript and is faster per request (median 1.04 s).
- **Not checked.** Any other caller that sends `temperature` to this model would get a 404. The dialogue's LLM fallback does not use this model by default (it names `mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct` in code, chosen on the dialogue's turn set, and sends 512 output tokens after a 30-token limit left a reasoning model's reply empty); the `--llm-model` option can name another.
- Caveats: text only (no speech, no STT output); scripted synthetic customers written by our team; Tamil and Kannada not human-reviewed; 70 of the 1,604 frozen replies also appear word for word in the development set; the reviewer's fixes have had no second independent review.

## 8. Engineering constraints

- **Concurrency 1** (429 "Only 1 active request", seen in a burst test on 2026-11-10): one queue for all requests, exponential backoff, no parallel REST calls. Bot audio is pre-generated, so a live call only uses STT. In the probe a WebSocket streaming session did not occupy that slot, but streaming was measured against REST per turn and is not shipped (decision 12 in §15, closed). A live turn waits 5 s for the key lock and then fails with `gnani_busy`, so nothing else may use the key during a call (§13). Read-backs of an amount the customer just said cannot be pre-generated: they are synthesised on the turn path (about 0.7 s of Timbre, `references/pilots/voice-loop-iter1.md` §8), while fixed prompts are cache hits.
- **Caching:** key = sha256(model, params, audio/text). Noise and 8 kHz variants are made offline, and only STT is re-run per condition.
- **Measured cost** (pilot): about 0.02 credits per 3-second STT call (n = 78) and 0.06 per short TTS sentence (n = 20), from one dashboard reading with no saved screenshot. The four-language plan (~1,350 TTS + ~7,400 STT) is about 230 to 265 credits, which is about a quarter of the 1,000-credit project cap.
- **Latency:** STT about 0.2 to 0.3 s and TTS about 0.6 to 0.8 s on short clips (pilot and client probe). Pre-generated prompts keep the bot responsive. In the amounts run (one run, one machine, not a benchmark) STT median was 0.21 s with p95 0.37 s on v2.5 and 0.33 s on v3.0. The second reading on an amount turn adds a call of about that size, and with concurrency 1 the two calls run one after the other. LLM extraction is not on this path: it runs after the call (§7).
- **Latency, phone end to end** (headless Chrome with a WAV as the microphone, real server, real Prisma v3.0; TTS clips only, no human speech): from the last voiced frame to the transcript in the page, a median 1.18 s (min 1.09, max 1.26; n = 12 turns from 5 Timbre clips and 1 stub answer, key lightly used and never idle). That is about 0.70 s of end-of-speech wait, 0.08 to 0.12 s of resampling and a 0.35 s median STT step. The 700 ms detector median was 685 ms on 20 clips plus silence (clean TTS against digital silence; a noisy room will differ). Streaming at its default 500 ms silence returned the transcript a median 0.78 s after the end of speech (n = 20), against about 0.71 s for the detector at 500 ms plus a REST call (n = 20 each). Source: `references/pilots/voice-loop-iter1.md` §3.
- **Backend proxy** (FastAPI, `samajh/server/`): browsers can't set `X-API-Key-ID`. The Gnani and OpenRouter keys live in environment variables only. The server resamples uploads to the 8 or 16 kHz line rate with ffmpeg; it binds 127.0.0.1, refuses other Host and Origin values, and has no login.
- **LLM hosting:** v1 sends only synthetic transcripts to OpenRouter. A real lender must check where transcripts may go first: for digital lenders, the Digital Lending Directions require storage in India and deletion within 24 hours of processing abroad (E2.9).
- **Synthetic data only:** obviously fake names, loan IDs, years of birth and numbers. Ombudsman cases are re-created with invented parties, never quoted with real names.

**What teach-back is, and what we don't claim for it.** Teach-back means asking a person to explain in their own words what they were told. It is well studied in healthcare. Reviews report benefits, but the newest systematic review (2026) found no clear pattern of effect on knowledge. We found no study of teach-back for loan or insurance sales, and none by an automated voice. E1 is our own test of it. Most of this rests on abstracts, not full papers (C3).

**Gold standard and circularity.** 11 of the 36 base cases are templated on public awards: five of the customer-relief awards in §1 (BHP-L-021-1617-0529, BNG-L-041-1617-0797, PUN-L-036-1617-0228, AHD-L-001-1617-0203, CHD-L-019-1718-1436), BNG-L-033-1617-0600 where the customer paid for a policy "as a requirement of a security deposit for the overdraft facility", BHP-L-013-1617-0436 where the complainant said he was asked to deposit ₹1 lakh "as an Fixed Deposit to get the housing loan", Baramulla consumer commission CC 33/2025 v J&K Bank where ₹2,00,000 was debited for a policy without consent (our copy of that order is a scan hosted by a news site), and three awards named nowhere else in this document: BHP-L-021-1617-0456, PUN-L-041-1617-0649 and PUN-L-009-1617-0009. PUN-L-017-1617-0684 (§1) is not used. Compliant scenarios are written synthetically from the rules: a required risk mitigant with a choice of provider, a voluntary add-on with a consent the customer recognises, a premium paid separately. Awards the insurer won are not used as compliant gold, because the complaint failed on evidence or delay and a teach-back-style call to those customers should still flag. Gold verdicts are written first and **frozen with a hash before any audio is made**. TTS results and human results are reported separately, and failure cases are published. Until human calls exist, every result is labelled TTS-only.

Each of the 11 templated base cases records the remedy in its source award: 5 refund, 4 conversion, 1 partial refund, 1 other. Of the 22 awards we read that gave the customer relief, about two-thirds ordered a refund and about one-third a conversion to a single-premium policy.

**The scenario set** (`data/scenarios/`, built by `samajh/scenarios/`; datasheet in `data/scenarios/README.md`, history in `CHANGELOG.md`). 144 synthetic scenarios: 36 hand-written base cases × 4 languages (hi, hi-en, ta, kn), frozen at version 3 (`gold.jsonl` sha256 beginning `14e49b58`). Each holds the record, what the customer believes, hand-written gold with a rationale, a scripted teach-back call and a scripted yes/no call. By gold verdict the 36 base cases are 9 clear, 25 flag for review and 2 unclear.
- **Agreement with the rule engine: 144 of 144 scenarios**, both on perfect beliefs and on the scripted-extraction path. **This was reached partly by a decision, not only by independent confirmation.** Gold written against the earlier reading of §6 disagreed with the engine on 8 scenarios (U09 and U10 in all four languages: the customer says they do not know or do not remember the premium; gold said `unclear`, the engine raised a medium `premium` flag). The coordinator resolved that one disagreement in the engine's favour (§15 item 17) and gold was changed by hand for those 8 scenarios. The change was made before any audio or LLM extraction was run on the set, so no audio or extraction had been scored against the earlier gold. Even so, 144 of 144 is not independent evidence that the engine is right on those 8. A further extraction-path miss (U01-ta: the amount parser read a Tamil word in "to be paid only once" as the number 1) was fixed in the parser, not in gold.
- **Review.** One independent model reviewer (it wrote its own verdicts with gold hidden and without the rule engine) agreed with 35 of 36 base cases at version 1 on the full flag and unclear sets (36 of 36 on the case verdict). The fixes after that review (version 2) and the premium change (version 3) have had no independent review. No human has reviewed any of the set, and Tamil and Kannada have no fluent reviewer.
- **Limits.** Speech is templated, one to three variants per answer, so results on the set are an upper bound for the text path. The set is built to exercise each rule, not to mirror how often each problem occurs, so no rate measured on it says anything about prevalence.
- **Use.** The frozen set is for final measurement only. Never use it to tune prompts, parsers or rules. Key customer audio by a hash of the utterance text, not by the hash of `calls.jsonl`.

## 12. Why we think this is not yet common (our read)

1. **The rule is new.** It was issued on 15 June 2026 and takes effect on 1 January 2027.
2. **A low-cost survey is not excluded.** 85Y names surveys as an example and sets no channel, so a low-cost survey is not excluded by the text. A low-cost survey is the likely default. That is our inference. We found no bank or industry statement on how banks will comply.
3. **Most voice-AI vendors lead with support, sales and collections.** Of 11 vendor sites checked on 10 November 2026, 10 list support, 9 sales and 8 collections. Seven also sell audit or compliance products, and some market adjacent post-sale checks (§11). None of those pages cites 85Y.
4. **It's harder than it looks (our view).** It needs product records, deterministic comparison, and correct Indian amounts. In our 20-sentence TTS pilot (one voice), Prisma's digit output ignored the fraction word in 8 transcripts across 5 sentences. In the 240-sentence set (TTS audio, phone) the naive digit reading was right on 9 of 60 fraction sentences on v2.5 and 54 of 60 on v3.0.

What Samajh adds to the adjacent products: an open question before any product is named, a deterministic comparison with the lender's record, an evidence log, and a measured evaluation.

Unknowns: banks may build it in-house or adapt survey bots, and we found no public statement from any bank on how it will run 85Y feedback. No regulator or survey we found reports how many loans carry bundled insurance. The numbers that exist count complaints.

16. **Amount turns get two Prisma readings.** Each amount-bearing customer turn is transcribed by both v2.5 and v3.0. Agreement is taken as the amount; disagreement, or a missing reading, always triggers a spoken read-back. The read-back remains for every critical amount regardless. Basis: on 240 TTS sentences over simulated phone audio, the two readings agreed on 200 and 199 of those were right; v3.0 alone held a wrong amount with no warning on 17 of 240. Cost: more STT calls on amount turns and exposure to v2.5's 504s (§13). How the dialogue applies it: `step(state, text, second_reading=...)`; the call acts on the first reading unless only the second brings a figure the customer has not just heard or rejected; `Turn.text` is the reading acted on, and the other is kept in `CallState.second_readings` with an `agreed` flag (`samajh.amounts.cross_check`). The read-back is spoken for every stated premium either way, so a disagreement changes which figure is read and adds no read-back. Tested with hand-written pairs only. The adapter that fetches the second reading (`expecting(state.position)` says when) is not written, and no real pair of Prisma transcripts has been played into `step`.
