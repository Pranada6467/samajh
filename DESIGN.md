# Samajh — design v3

> A post-sale understanding callback for Indian lenders: an open-question comprehension check. The customer explains in their own words, in their own language, what they bought with their loan. A language model turns the answers into fields, and plain code compares them with the bank's records. Where an answer conflicts with the record, the case becomes evidence a human can review; gaps in understanding are counted in the half-yearly report.

## 1. The problem

A gold-loan borrower complained that ₹52,000 had been deducted from his loan amount, and that the bank had described it as a "fixed deposit for loan security". It was a life-insurance policy. The Insurance Ombudsman found he had asked for cancellation within two months and ordered the insurer to cancel the policy and refund the premium (Bhopal, BHP-L-021-1617-0529). The award does not mention a verification call; it turned on the free-look objection. It also states only the ₹52,000: the loan size and the net amount received are not in it.

Insurers already make verification calls, and in some awards the recorded call did not save the insurer:
- BNG-L-041-1617-0797: the insurer submitted the recording of the customer confirming the policy. The customer said the bank had assured him the money could be withdrawn after a year. The Ombudsman held the insurer responsible for its agent and ordered the policy converted.
- PUN-L-036-1617-0228: "The PIVC recording is clear about the terms and conditions of the policy but the maturity benefit is not explained." Refund ordered.
- PUN-L-017-1617-0684: the recording showed the caller's "main focus ... was on processing the application". Relief ordered: a refund by cancelling the policies, and the policy of the complainant's wife converted to a single-premium policy.

In four awards the complainant said they had been told how to answer the call. One was told "there would be a verification call and in reply she had to answer everything in positive" (AHD-L-001-1617-0203). In another the Ombudsman accepted that the customer "responded as was guided" (CHD-L-019-1718-1436).

A recorded "yes" is weak evidence of understanding. It is not worthless. Insurers have won where the recording showed the caller stating the disputed point and the customer agreeing (PUN-L-013-1617-0583), and where the Ombudsman heard the recording and the customer had confirmed the proposal details (NOI-L-036-1819-0337). In the first award the complaint was first raised about a year after the policy bond was received (B-N14); in the second the complainant asked to cancel after 19 months (B-N15). Two awards are not a rate, and a dismissal is not a finding that the sale was compliant (B-N14 note).

## 2. The rule that makes this timely

On 15 June 2026 RBI issued ten entity-specific Amendment Directions on "Advertising, Marketing and Sale of Financial Products and Services by Regulated Entities". They take effect on **1 January 2027**. Commercial banks: Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks - Responsible Business Conduct) Second Amendment Directions, 2026, RBI/2026-27/115, para 85Y. NBFCs: RBI/2026-27/123, para 101Y. Small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, urban and rural co-operative banks and All India Financial Institutions have the same paragraph under their own numbers. Housing finance companies follow NBFC paragraphs 101A to 101ZA through para 157A of the HFC Directions (RBI/2026-27/124). RBI's consolidated Directions pages did not yet show paragraphs 85A to 85ZA on 10 November 2026, so we cite the amendment.

> "85Y. A bank shall establish a mechanism to seek feedback from customers, within a period of 30 days from the sale of any financial product / service to ensure that customers have understood the features of the financial product / service and also the risks associated with such financial product / service. The mechanism may include selection of the customers on a random basis for such feedback through modes such as call-backs or surveys carried out by a department / vertical of the bank not associated with sale of products / services. A half-yearly report on the findings of the feedback shall be prepared and utilised for review of existing policies and features of financial products / services."

Samajh is a tool a bank's non-sales unit can use to run that mechanism.

85Y requires four things: a feedback mechanism, feedback sought within 30 days of the sale, the purpose of ensuring customers understood features and risks, and a half-yearly report used to review policies and products. Random selection, call-backs, surveys and the independent unit sit in a sentence that begins "may include". 85Y does not say who receives the report and requires no filing with RBI.

We add checks against the related paragraphs of the same chapter to the 85Y callback. 85Y doesn't require these checks, but the bank must meet those paragraphs anyway, and the evidence falls out of the same call:

| Para (commercial banks) | What it says | What we check |
|---|---|---|
| 85G | Explicit consent for each product (for example a signed declaration, OTP, digitally recorded confirmation, or a demarcated clause), kept until one year after the contract ends | Is there a consent record for each product, and does the customer recognise it? |
| 85V | No compulsory bundling of a third-party product with the bank's own. A product required as a risk mitigant is allowed if the customer may buy it from any provider | Was the product required for the loan? If so, is it a risk mitigant, and was the customer offered a choice of provider? |
| 85W | No paying for a product out of the loan without explicit consent | Was the premium deducted from the loan, and did the customer know? |
| 4(20A)(ii) | Mis-selling includes incorrect, incomplete or misleading information | Does what the customer believes contradict the product's features? A wrong belief is evidence that incorrect information may have been given, not proof |
| 85Z | Where mis-selling is established, the bank refunds the entire amount paid and compensates any loss as per its approved policy. A customer may also complain within the timeline set by the product's regulator or, where none is set, within 30 days of receiving the signed terms | We prepare an evidence pack for the bank's reviewer. A flag does not trigger 85Z |

For NBFCs the sub-paragraph letters are the same and the prefix changes: 101G, 101V, 101W, 101Y, 101Z. The mis-selling definition is para 6(9A) for NBFCs, not 4(20A) (A5, A19).

RBI does not define "risk mitigant". Treating credit-life cover on a loan as one is our reading. A savings policy described as a "security deposit" or an "FD" is not a risk mitigant on any reading (A16).

## 3. Users

- **Primary:** the department of a bank or NBFC that doesn't sell products and runs the 85Y feedback mechanism (customer experience, compliance or audit).
- **Secondary:** the compliance head who owns the half-yearly report; voice-agent vendors (Gnani included) who sell to these lenders.

Insurance sold with a loan is a "third-party product or service" that the bank sells under an agency or referral arrangement with the provider, so the bank owns the duties of this chapter for that sale (A24).

## 4. Scope

**In scope**
- One product family: a loan (personal or gold) sold with credit-life / loan-protection insurance.
- Languages: **Hindi and Hinglish are the product languages.** Tamil and Kannada stay in the code as experimental: they appear only in an appendix, get no new effort, and are not in the demo or any headline. A language counts as **supported** only with a fluent reviewer for its scripts and scenarios and ≥10 improvised real-voice calls. Today no human recording exists, so Hindi and Hinglish are "TTS-only, not human-tested". Regional languages are our choice: 85R covers documents for the bank's own products and defers to the product regulator for third-party products, so, on our reading, it does not ground the language choice for insurance (para 85R of the commercial banks' directions; the claims ledger has no row of its own for 85R: row A25 is suitability, and 85R appears only in the note under it).
- One outbound call flow, from a simulated phone in the browser.
- Checks: 85Y understanding, 85G, 85V, 85W, and belief mismatch (which may engage 4(20A)(ii)).
- Outputs: a case file per call with a triage tier, what a referral rests on (the customer's words or the lender's record alone) and a short summary; a reviewer queue; a half-yearly report page built from a batch of calls, with the reach funnel. All over a synthetic loan book.

**Not in scope**
- Real telephony and real bank integrations (all records and calls are synthetic). The number series and telecom declarations in §5 describe a real deployment; the demo does not exercise them.
- Other products and a second product family (the lender's own terms are the natural next one: a slide, not a build).
- Analysing the original sales call.
- Verifying who the caller is. The call does a right-party check (§5). It is not identity verification, and no line, page or write-up says it is.
- Deciding whether something *is* mis-selling: we only produce evidence for human review.
- Testing whether the product was suitable for the customer (4(20A)(i)).
- Re-teaching: Samajh assesses understanding and does not explain the product.
- Live streaming STT: REST per turn with a browser end-of-speech detector (decision 12 in §15, closed by measurement).
- A Key Facts Statement check, an SMS arm, a dark-pattern slide (dropped, §15).

## 5. The call

This section describes the call as P2 built it (`references/pilots/P2-contract.md` sections 5 to 7 are as built; the code wins). The P2 fix rounds and iteration 3's fix round have landed: the turn classifier is the primary judge at the opening, a who-check follows any sign of another speaker, and the right-party setting is a fixed menu (P8). An independent verifier's latest check (`references/reviews/iter3/verify-call.md`: 151 new typed Hindi and Hinglish inputs, then 78 more after the fix round, classifier live and failing) gave the verdict **ready for consenting testers playing customers, with the classifier on and degraded sessions voided; not ready for anyone else.** All of it is typed text, not speech. The numbers and the conditions are in §13.

```
right-party check → short opening and go-ahead → second check (year of birth; only if the lender sets it) → context → open recall
  → questions per product (or the "took nothing" branch) → consent check → close
  (read-backs follow the premium and a stated cover)
```

The second check is a lender setting, default none (step 3; decided 2026-11-10, built in iteration 3). With the year of birth on, an ordinary "yes" at step 1 puts the year before the opening lines.

1. **Right-party check.** "Am I speaking with <first name>?" An explicit first-person claim ("हाँ जी, बोल रहा हूँ", "मैं ही हूँ") passes at once and goes on to the opening. An ordinary confirmation ("हाँ जी", "बोलिए") gets the direct question "are you <first name> yourself?" (`right_party.reask`); with the default setting (no second check) a yes to it passes, and with the year of birth on it goes to the year-of-birth question (step 3). A relation word, another name, a hedge ("वही समझ लीजिए") or a question at either step is not a pass. A speaker who says or implies they are someone else ("रमेश की वाइफ़", "Ramesh's brother") ends the call as `wrong_party` with a line that gives only the first name and "customer care". A refusal to talk ends it as `declined` (`do_not_call` when the words ask for that); the borrower away plus a stop ends `wrong_party`. Verifier's wording: "26 of 26 opening refusals ended as asked with the classifier live (25 of 26 failing)." Anything else gets one re-ask. The loan is never named at this step. **Later in the call**, a sign of another speaker (a relation word, a third-person word, a restart such as "what were you asking?", or a question such as "whom do you want?") gets the who-check (`right_party.who_check`: "are you <first name> yourself, or someone else in their place?"). The call resumes only on an explicit self-claim; a bare yes, a relative's answer or anything else ends it as `wrong_party`. Signs are read at the go-ahead as well, and by design no line that names the lender, the loan or a figure plays while a sign is unsettled (`close.verify` while a sign is unsettled is a declared cost and was not measured). Verifier's wording: "A bare yes at the who-check ended the call in every case that reached it (8 of 8 live, 10 of 10 failing)." and "On 78 new verifier-written typed inputs, every takeover sign after the pass (17 of 17, each arm) got the who-check or an end with no lender, loan or figure spoken after it." **Known and accepted:** a third party who answers with a false "हाँ" twice or a false "हाँ मैं ही हूँ" passes, and no text rule can catch that (the default treats a person who says yes twice as the borrower: 5 of 5 such false claimants passed). The costs fall on genuine borrowers (§13). This is a right-party check, not identity verification.
2. **Opening.** After the yes, four short lines: who is calling (an automated assistant from the lender's customer care), that this is feedback and not sales, that the call is recorded with a pointer to the lender's written notice, and "may we talk for two or three minutes?" That is 48 words before the customer's second turn, 19.2 s at an assumed 2.5 words a second (a word count of the round 2 lines, not recomputed after rounds 3 and 4 or iteration 3). In the one completed practice call by microphone (one speaker) the first substantive answer began at 54.7 s of 141 s (a reviewer's reading of the session log, `references/reviews/iter2/product-review.md` §2). A no ends the call as `declined`; a request for a later call as `call_back_later`. "How to check that the call is genuine" (hang up and call the number printed on the papers from the lender) is said on request and at every completed close.
3. **Second check, no secret: a lender setting, default none (decided by the user on 2026-11-10; built in iteration 3 as P8; verified on typed text).** The setting is a fixed menu in code (`samajh/identity/policy.py`); a lender picks from it and no field holds free text, so no deployment can make the bot ask a question the code does not contain. `second_check`: none or `year_of_birth` (default none). `attempts`: 1 or 2, two only with the year (default 1). `prior_notice`: a written message before the call (default on; **mocked** in the demo, never sent). `call_back_line`: the "hang up and call the number printed on your papers" line (default on). A PIN-code check and any second item are not built (no Indian source shows either as practice, ledger I4). **With none, a bare yes is not enough:** the caller must also say yes to "are you <first name> yourself?" (step 1), and the setting's description, the case file and the demo page say that a person who affirms being the borrower is treated as the borrower and that this is a right-party check, not identity verification. **With `year_of_birth`:** one low-sensitivity fact, the year of birth (synthetic in the demo), asked with the line "we will never ask for an OTP, PIN, password or account number". The bot never asks for account digits. A near-spelling of the same year passes (5 of 6 slips passed in `verify-P2-r4.md`; one, "उनीस सौ चोरासी", was re-asked and not passed in `verify-call.md`, A-y09), a reply with no year gets one re-ask, a hedged year ("1984 के आसपास") is a guess and uses an attempt, and a different year ends the call as `second_check_failed`. With two attempts a second wrong answer ends the call; verifier's wording: "With two attempts, 5 of 5 pairs of wrong years ended the call." A two-digit year passes only when the last two digits of the year on record are 10 or more. A speaker who says they are someone else fails the check even with the right year. Failure ends the call with the loan not named (`second_check_failed`, or `wrong_party`). Under the old always-on check, one of the two practice calls ended here: a 280 ms sound gave no words, the bot re-asked and the user hung up.

   **What the check protects and what it does not.** It stops a stranger who falsely claims the name and does not know the year. It does not stop a false claimant who gives the right year: those passed 3 of 3 in the verifier's inputs (`verify-P2-r3.md`), and again 3 of 3 in `verify-call.md`. Family, or anyone holding a leaked record, can answer it (our reading; ledger IX5). It costs 15 to 20 s. Four institutions publish advice that bears on a date of birth on a call (Axis Bank, SBI Life, Kotak, LIC; ledger I3), and RBI's booklet says impostors on calls share the customer's name or date of birth to gain confidence (I2), so the question can look like the scam customers are warned about. On the first-person path the lender is already named in the opening line, so the step protects only the word "loan" (the judge's reading, `judge.md` §4(c); and in the year-of-birth setting a customer who answers the name question with an explicit claim hears the lender named before the year, and `end.check_failed` names it again: `verify-call.md` C-y08, not fixed; a lender may still want the step). No RBI, TRAI or IRDAI text we found prescribes how to confirm who is on an outbound call (I1), and no lender publishes its data points (IX2). The check is a right-party check, never "identity verified".

   **Where it is set and what is recorded.** `samajh/server/settings.toml` `[identity]`, read through `parse_policy` (anything outside the menu fails at load); a phone session may carry a top-level `identity` object (422 outside the menu: 14 invalid forms got 422 in the verifier's inputs). The demo page has a picker (None / Year of birth, attempts 1 or 2) with the description under it and shows the mocked written notice ("SMS, mocked: not sent"). Routes: `GET /api/identity/menu`, `GET /api/identity/notice`. The case file gains `identity` (which check, attempts allowed and used, passed, notice shown and mocked, one fixed sentence; no field for an answer) and `identity_description`; if a later takeover ends the call as a wrong party, the sentence says so. The expected year never reached a classifier prompt, a bot line, the identity record or an end-of-call record in the verifier's inputs. Verifier's wording: "The case file withholds the reply to the year question and the Batch segments that overlap it in time; the on-disk dialogue state and the audio still hold it." Also not withheld: the demo player's cheat sheet (`/api/records`, "born 1984"). The Batch withholding works by time window, not by content: a segment that drifts off the turn on real audio would keep the words (not tested). **Built, not verified:** the demo page was not opened in a browser by the verifier; the notice texts (`samajh/identity/notices/`) have no fluent reader, and the Tamil and Kannada notices are `not human-reviewed`. Research and contract: `references/pilots/identity-check-research.md`, `P8-contract.md`; the setting as built: `P8-report.md`.
4. **Context.** Only now does the bot mention the loan, and "you can stop, or ask for a person, at any time" comes with the first mention.
5. **Open recall (the key question).** The bot asks whether anything was taken with the loan and, if so, what and for how much. It names no product, so it does not lead the answer, and it does not presuppose that anything was taken. A customer who says they took nothing ("कुछ नहीं लिया, बस लोन लिया था") gets one neutral follow-up, then the consent question and the close; no product question is asked. The denial is recorded and the rules decide what it means (§6).
6. **Questions for each product on record.** The bot never names the product's kind, so no spoken line holds the words insurance, policy, premium, cover, FD or deposit (the lint in `samajh/dialogue/lint.py` rejects them). With one product it says "the thing taken with the loan"; with several, "the first / second thing". Per product, in this order: what the customer thinks it is, in their own words; what they paid (then the read-back, step 8); where and how the money was paid; one payment or recurring; whether it was needed for the loan or their choice; what they were told about where to take it from (**asked only when the customer does not say it was their own choice**; the branch depends on the customer's answer, not on the record, so it leaks nothing); what it gives and up to what amount (a stated figure is read back); how long it runs; whether it can be cancelled and until when; and the open money-back question ("if you wanted this money back, what would happen?"), which is how a belief such as "the money can be withdrawn after a year" (BNG-L-041-1617-0797) comes out. It is the call's one risk probe; its limit is stated in §15 item 39. "I don't know" is an answer and is never re-asked; a bare yes or no to an either/or question is asked again once. The bot compares the cancel answer with `cancel_window_days` on the record and never states a right itself.
7. **Consent check.** "When you took the loan, what did you say yes to, and how did you say yes?" (the lint bans "buy" and any suggested method such as "sign" or "form"). The bot compares the answer with the consent record. The call never *captures* consent.
8. **Read-back.** The premium the customer stated is read back at once, in their language, with the figure the customer said and never the record's, and with what it is for ("तो आपने उसके लिए बावन हज़ार रुपये दिए, यानी 52,000। सही है?"). A cover figure the customer states is read back the same way. A hedged or ranged premium ("पचास बावन हज़ार के आसपास") is read back as said, with both ends (a range is not read back for cover). A reply that names another figure gets "so what is the right amount?"; "I don't know" is recorded as unresolved and not pressed. At most three read-backs per product. Why: Prisma v3.0 writes some amounts confidently wrong (§9, E3).
9. **Close.** Thanks, the pointer to check the call is genuine, and "our team may call again" only when the live call left something unresolved. Flags are computed after the call, so the bot has no flag to announce and promises nothing. The close mentions no other product.

**Two readings on figure turns.** Where the dialogue expects a figure, the server also fetches the second Prisma reading (v2.5 `transcribe`, bounded at 2.5 s; a late or failed reading counts as missing) and passes both to `step(..., second_reading=...)`. If both give the same figure, it is read back at once. If they differ or one holds no figure, the bot asks the customer to say the amount once more (once per product), then reads back. At a read-back, one yes and one no repeat the read-back. `Turn.text` holds the reading the call acted on and the other is kept with an `agreed` flag for the evidence. **The order: v2.5 first when it has text.** `readings()` in `samajh/server/samajh_dialogue.py` passes the v2.5 reading first when it has text, and the v3.0 reading first when v2.5 is missing, empty, late or failed, as P2's contract asks (iteration 1 verifier H4: on the 240 real Prisma pairs of the development amounts set, TTS audio, in-sample, the held figure was wrong 2 times with v2.5 first against 16 with v3.0 first). The P1b verifier found 30 of 30 independent pairs consistent across session JSON, dialogue state and case file (`verify-P1b-r2.md`). A known trade-off: a refusal heard only by v3.0 is not acted on when v2.5 has text. Closed (§15 item 34).

**Unclear, refusals and ends.** An unclear answer is asked once more in simpler words, then recorded as unresolved (one re-ask also avoids the "nagging" pattern in RBI's dark-pattern annex, A20). The bot never argues with the customer and never explains the product during the call: that would contaminate the test. Thirteen outcomes: `completed`, `wrong_party`, `not_confirmed`, `no_go_ahead`, `second_check_failed`, `declined`, `human_requested`, `turn_limit`, `call_back_later`, `language_switch` (the call ends and records the language asked for; there is no live switch), `no_response` (two silent turns in a row), `do_not_call` (the customer asked not to be called again), `written_requested` (the customer asked for it in writing instead). A refusal, a request for a person or a request for a later call ends the call (without a stated time the bot asks "when should we call?" once). "Who is this?" and "is this a fraud call?" get the verification lines and the question again, twice at most. "Be quick" continues the call. A third-person or habitual narration ("वो लोग हमेशा बोलते हैं") makes refusal words an answer, because those are the sentences the product exists to hear. Limits so a call always ends: one re-ask per question, at most 60 bot utterances, 3 read-backs per product, 2 stop questions, 2 silences. An LLM turn classifier (Mistral small, 2.5 s wall-clock limit) works beside the word lists and is the primary judge at the opening; on a failure the turn keeps the word lists' reading and is marked degraded. It has to be on for every call, and a session with a degraded turn is to be voided (`verify-call.md`: "with the classifier on and degraded sessions voided"; §13). A neutral acknowledgement "जी।" (`ack.neutral`) is said at fixed positions whatever the answer; the verifier notes that it is a yes-word and that a customer who gave a wrong answer can hear it as agreement, and asks a native speaker to choose between "हम्म" and no acknowledgement (§13).

**The call keeps no tested value.** `start` keeps a plan (loan id, first name, birth year, product ids) and nothing else from the record; with no second check the plan's birth year is None (83 of 83 verifier calls) and the lender's setting is the only other thing `start` takes (`identity=IdentityPolicy()`). No line can depend on the kind, premium, payment source, cover, term, cancel window, consent or provider choice, and the only figures the bot says are ones the customer said first. Test: two records that differ in every tested value got word-for-word identical bot lines (random customers in four languages, and random-interpreter calls; the test is extended to the new branches). The bot's words do show that a product is on record and how many.

**Call length.** An informed one-product call is 147.6 s by word count (bot plus customer, assumed 2.5 words a second; `call_seconds`), under the 150 s target by 2.4 s. A misled customer who talks more ran 184.8 s. These are word counts of the round 2 lines, not recomputed after rounds 3 and 4 or iteration 3. The one completed practice call by microphone (one speaker, the user) lasted 141 s (`product-review.md`).

**What grounds the opening.**
- The call is made from the lender's 1600-series number. Required for service and transactional calls: TRAI direction of 19 November 2025 para 8(iii); RBI/2024-25/105. No TRAI text classifies a feedback call. We treat it as a service call, and it carries no promotional content (promotional content needs the 140 series and consent under the TCCCPR).
- The lender tells its telecom provider in advance that it uses automated calls: TCCCPR regulation 4 as substituted in February 2025. The Third Amendment of 18 September 2026 widens this to a declaration of all application-to-person calls, from 60 days after gazette publication.
- The AI line and the offer of a human: recommended by RBI's FREE-AI committee report, not required by an RBI direction (para 4.4.54, read in a mirror copy; E4.3). We found no Indian law or RBI direction that requires them for a lender's own outbound call (E4.4, E4.8: an absence search, not proof).
- The recording line: our choice. The only RBI recording duty we found is for recovery calls.
- The purpose, the right to decline, and the pointer to a written notice: required by the DPDP Act sections 5 and 6 from 13 May 2027 where the lender relies on consent.
- Calls are placed between 10:00 and 19:00: our choice (85N(4) sets 09:00 to 19:00 for sales staff and the card rules 10:00 to 19:00).
- Why the opening changed from design v1: v1 asked for loan-account digits and a date of birth and named the loan before confirming who had answered. RBI's public advice says never to share account details, PIN or OTP with anyone; an unknown caller asking for them looks like the pattern it describes (E5.2, E5.3), and naming the loan first would disclose it to whoever answers. No RBI, TRAI or IRDAI text we found prescribes how to confirm who is on an outbound call (E5.1, I1). A first-name "yes" alone is a weak check, which is why the direct question exists (step 1) and the year of birth is a lender setting (step 3).

## 6. How it works

```
LOAN RECORD (synthetic) ─► DIALOGUE (samajh/dialogue + data/prompts/: line templates, no value under
                           test is kept) ─► bot lines (Timbre v3.0, 8 kHz; fixed lines pre-made,
                           read-backs made on the turn because they carry the customer's figure)
customer audio, each turn ─► PRISMA REST (v3.0 transcribe; on figure turns v2.5 transcribe as well)
                           ─► AMOUNT NORMALISER (fraction-aware; the two readings compared)
        after the call ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
whole call ─► PRISMA BATCH (segment times, speakers) ─► aligned to the customer's turns (Batch text is not what is read)
transcript (the dialogue's own turn texts: v3.0, and v2.5 on figure turns) ─► EXTRACTOR (LLM, strict schema, a quoted span per field; requests in parallel)
           ─► RULE ENGINE (pure functions: answer vs record ─► flags and gaps) ─► TIER ─► CASE SUMMARY
case file (tier, summary, every quote with turn id and audio offset, both readings of each figure,
models and parameters, audio hash) ─► REVIEWER QUEUE (`refer` cases) · HALF-YEARLY REPORT (all tiers + reach funnel)
```

**Core types (sketch; `samajh/types.py` wins)**
```python
# The lender's record (all frozen; unknown fields rejected)
Product    = {id, kind: "credit_life", provider, premium_inr, cover_inr, term_months,
              paid_from_loan, required_for_loan, risk_mitigant, provider_choice_offered,
              cancel_window_days | None,

              disbursed_inr, disbursed_on, tenure_months | None, products: [Product]}

# What the customer said: every answer is exactly one of four states
Answer[T]  = NotAsked | Unclear(reason) | DontKnow(quote, turn_id) | Stated(value, quote, turn_id,
                                          hedged, approximate, low, also)
# Two layers: the LLM only fills the first; deterministic code builds the second
RawExtraction -> Beliefs     # rupees, months, days parsed; every quote found in the right customer turn

Flag = {rule_id, reason: FlagReason, clause, product_id, severity, expected, heard, quote, turn_id, also}
Gap  = {rule_id, reason: GapReason, clause, product_id, quote, turn_id}

                                                                # FlagReason its basis; referral_basis(flags) reads refer-tier flags only
```

The paragraph a rule may engage is looked up by lender type (A5, A19): `kind`, `premium`, `cover`, `term`, `cancel_window`, `recall`, `recall_unmatched` engage 85Y (101Y for NBFCs); `consent` 85G (101G); `bundling` 85V (101V); `loan_funded` 85W (101W); `belief_mismatch` 4(20A)(ii) (6(9A)(ii)); `sale_conduct` and `evidence` are `internal`, engaging no paragraph. `Verdict` (`clear | flag_for_review | unclear_needs_human`) stays because the frozen gold is written in it; new code reads `tier`.

**Rules.** Deterministic; the LLM is never called. The paragraph in brackets is the commercial-bank one:
- `kind` (85Y): the stated kind ≠ the record ("FD" vs credit-life) → flag. "I don't know what it is" → flag. No usable answer → gap.
- `premium` (85Y): beyond 1% of the record → flag; "I don't know" → flag (medium). The premium **passes only after the customer confirms a read-back of the same figure** (for a range, a read-back with the same two ends). Within the approximate tolerance (10%, range-aware) and confirmed → `premium_approximate` (low). Further off and confirmed → `premium_differs_confirmed`; unconfirmed → `premium_differs` (it may be a mis-hearing). A confirmed range wider than twice its low end never passes (`not_comparable` gap).
- `cover` (85Y): within 1% and unhedged passes. A plain figure beyond tolerance is `cover_differs_confirmed` once the customer confirmed a read-back of that figure and `cover_differs_unconfirmed` until then; hedged or approximate, or "I don't know", is a low flag.
- `term`, `cancel_window` (85Y): unaware or different → low flag; "I don't know" → low flag; the cancel "by when" is compared in days with 1 day of tolerance; the code never states a right. "As long as the loan runs" is compared with `tenure_months` on the record, else a `not_comparable` gap.
- `recall` (85Y): asked what they took, the customer names no product on the record → low flag (`recall_not_named`). A customer who says they took nothing → `recall_denied` (high) while the denial stands; if the customer later names the product kind on the record, it becomes `recall_not_named` with both quotes.
- `recall_unmatched` (85Y, case-level): an insurance, deposit or investment named that is not on the record → flag (medium).
- `consent` (85G): no consent on record → flag. A record on file passes only when the customer's own account includes this product **and** names the same method of agreeing. A product not recognised → flag (high); "I don't know what I agreed to" → flag; a different method → flag (medium); agrees but cannot say how → gap.
- `loan_funded` (85W): `paid_from_loan` and (no consent, or the customer says it was paid separately or did not know) → flag. The record says paid separately and the customer says deducted → flag. Record says separately and the customer does not know → gap (§15 item 19).
- `bundling` (85V): the product was required (per the record or the customer) **and** at least one of: the record says it is not a risk mitigant; the customer describes it as a deposit (not a risk mitigant on any reading, A16); the record shows no choice of provider; the customer says they were not told they could buy it elsewhere. Each reason is its own flag (high).
- `belief_mismatch` (may engage 4(20A)(ii)): a belief contradicts the product's `features` (refundable, guaranteed return, FD, or a single payment when the record does not list `one_time_payment`; the payment-frequency question must have been asked for that last one) → flag. The money-back question must be asked: without a usable answer the product is at best a gap, and "I don't know" is a low flag. The paragraph is shown as the paragraph the flag may engage, never as a verdict.
- `sale_conduct` (internal, case-level): the customer voices a concern about how the sale or the confirmation was handled (told what to answer, learned of the product only after the sale) → flag (high).
- `evidence` (internal): a quote that is not in the call transcript supports no verdict → gap.

**Answers the rules read with care** (`P3a-contract.md`): a hedge ("करीब", "शायद", "I think") or a narrow range ("पचास बावन हज़ार") is read as (low, high), marked, never a pass by itself; a correction, a negator in the same clause, "and", three ends, or a range wider than twice its low end stays refused. A question asked twice keeps every answer; **two stated, incompatible answers** (beyond 1% for amounts, or 10% when hedged or ranged) become an `answers_conflict` flag, while "I don't know" before or after an answer, a restatement, or an approximate and an exact figure inside it are not conflicts. **Retractions.** The rule the code applies: when a later clause of the same reply takes back a stated answer ("…, ऐसा तो नहीं था", "नहीं नहीं", "galat bola", "actually no"), the taken-back value is not kept as the stated answer; the answer is a gap, and a conflict if a different answer follows. A model reads the reply for this (the change check). Since iteration 3 the check runs on a reply of two or more clauses and also on a one-clause reply that holds a take-back word; on a one-clause reply a `withdrawn`, `corrected` or failed check makes the answer unusable (`incomplete`). A code rule needs no model: a one-clause reply that ends in a "who knows / forgot / or something else" tail is unusable whatever the check said, unless an exactness word comes just before the tail ("ठीक से याद नहीं") or, on a question that is not about an amount, the reply names another detail ("बीमा है पता नहीं कितने का"). The carried premium follows the same rules. No prompt text changed; sha256 tests now pin the prompts and wire schemas, so an edit fails a test (a one-character edit to the `ChangeReply` docstring failed 2 of 5 pin tests). **What the verifier measured** (`references/reviews/iter3/verify-chain.md`, typed text, not speech), in its wording: "On 23 typed withdrawals that hold a marker word (one-clause, punctuated or carried from recall), none ended `clear`." "On 19 typed informed replies where नहीं/nahi/no is the answer, or with an apology or filler, 19 ended `clear`." "On 10 typed corrections away from the record, the taken-back value was never the stated answer." "On 5 typed punctuated 'who knows' withdrawals, none ended `clear`." "On 7 typed bounded premiums, none ended `clear` and the bound was never taken as the premium." **Known limits.** The verifier's verdicts were "sound with conditions, with one regression to fix before use" and, after the first fix round, "holds with conditions". Its inputs ran before the last fix round, which answered the recheck's four items and was **not re-verified** (`references/pilots/P3a-report.md`, "Iteration 3 (chain)"). Before that round: 10 of 13 one-clause withdrawals with no marker word ended `clear` with the value the customer took back (round 1); 2 of 13 with a "who knows / forgot" tail still did (both ended in "या फिर कुछ और"); a romanised count before an ordinary noun ("do kisht mein 41300") was read as an amount; and 9 of 13 informed one-clause replies with a tail about another field or about exactness were falsely made `incomplete`. The builder's rerun of the verifier's own inputs after the last round reads 0 and 0, which is not evidence because the builder knew them. One-clause corrections, even toward the record, are `incomplete` (5 of 5 on the verifier's probe; the accepted cost). A withdrawal phrased with a word on no list still rests on one model reading, and the tail rule covers one-clause replies only. The protection of the carried premium rests on one model reading: rerun the retraction probes after any edit to the change check's prompt. **How often the text is one clause** (`eval/text_path/punctuation_rate.py`, cache only, the extractor's own clause splitter; `P3a-report.md`): v2.5 `transcribe` over six words was one clause in 246 of 246 public real-voice clips and in 376 of 395 Timbre clips; v3.0 `transcribe` over six words had two or more clauses in 104 of 248 real-voice clips and 26 of 392 Timbre clips. Figure-turn text, which the case file reads from v2.5 (see the diagram above), is almost always one clause. The earlier "640 of 640" in `prisma-punctuation-note.md` counted any sentence-ending mark, final full stops included, and overstated how often the change check could run (the note is corrected). The counts cover only replies of more than six words, and no customer speech is in them. A failed model call on a reply makes only its own fields `Unclear(extraction_failed)`; a failed screening call becomes a case-level gap. Silence is never a pass: a question never asked or not usably answered is a gap.

**Tiers** (the one table is `FLAG_TIER` in `samajh/types.py`; the code wins):

| Tier | Meaning | Flags | Gaps under rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `refer` | a human must look; goes in the reviewer queue | `kind_*`, `premium_differs_confirmed`, `cover_differs_confirmed`, `recall_denied`, `recall_off_record`, `consent_*`, `funded_*`, `unfunded_says_deducted`, `bundled_*`, `feature_contradicted`, `sale_concern`, `answers_conflict` on these rules | none |
| `incomplete` | the call did not yield enough to decide | `premium_differs`, `cover_differs_unconfirmed` | `kind`, `premium`, `consent`, `loan_funded`, `bundling`, `belief_mismatch`, `recall_unmatched`, `sale_conduct`, `evidence` |
| `understanding_gap` | reported in aggregate in the half-yearly report, not queued | `premium_dont_know`, `premium_approximate`, `cover_differs`, `cover_dont_know`, `term_*`, `cancel_*`, `recall_not_named`, `feature_dont_know`, `frequency_*`, `answers_conflict` on cover, term or window | `cover`, `term`, `cancel_window`, `recall` |
| `clear` | every rule passed | none | none |

A product's tier is the worst of its flags and gaps; a case's tier is the worst of its products and the case-level ones. Term and the cancellation window stay `understanding_gap` even when the customer states a wrong figure (coordinator decision, §15 item 28). A flag is evidence for review, never a finding: no line says "mis-selling" or "refund", and the 85Z refund duty arises only where mis-selling is established (A18), which a flag is not.

**What a referral rests on (P3c, built).** A tag beside the tier, never a tier: it changes no tier, verdict, flag or gap (the raw results of all 144 frozen rows were byte-identical before and after, and `samajh.scenarios verify` passes). `FLAG_BASIS` in `samajh/types.py` gives every flag reason a basis. Four reasons rest on **the lender's record alone** (`consent_no_record`, `funded_no_consent`, `bundled_not_mitigant`, `bundled_no_choice_on_record`); a missing consent on the record needs no call to find. The other 34 rest on **what the customer said**. One exception: the two bundling reasons rest on the customer's words when the record does not mark the product as required and only the customer said it was. A referral's basis is `customer_statement` when at least one refer-tier flag rests on the customer's words, and `record_only` otherwise. Why: so that "referrals per 100" describes the customers and not only the lender's database; the judge and the product review asked for it (`judge.md` §4(b), `product-review.md` §2.1). A referral on the record alone starts with one sentence, "Referred on the lender's record alone; nothing the customer said is the reason for the referral." The reviewer queue can be filtered by basis, and the half-yearly report gives every referral count as two counts side by side ("a on what the customer said · b on the record alone"). Either kind is a request for a person to look, and nothing more. A referral on the record alone does not mean the customer understood, and no record-only rate is a fact about a lender. On the frozen text set, Hindi and Hinglish: all 30 problem referrals rest on what the customer said; of the 16 informed customers with a fault on the lender's record, 8 were referred, 6 on what the customer said (3 base cases: G03, V02, V03) and 2 on the record alone (1 base case: W01). So the split moves 2 of the 8 and not all of them. The demo batch (64 analysed, 7 referred) has 0 on the record alone: the book produces no record-only referral, so the demo does not exercise that side. **State:** built by one builder, then one narrow check by a separate subagent that wrote none of it, which recounted both sets with its own classification and agreed on every number and every split; its six defects were fixed, and those fixes were confirmed by the coordinating worker only (tests, frozen comparison, demo). There is no saved verification report for P3c in `references/reviews/iter2/`; the pages were re-rendered on 2026-11-13 (from the working tree, with other workers' edits) and show the split; the fixes were not independently re-checked: **built, not independently verified.** Details: `references/pilots/P3a-report.md` (P3c) and `P3a-contract.md`.

**The case summary and the evidence.** `samajh.rules.summarise(record, result)` returns what a reviewer reads: one line per distinct `refer` issue and one line per rule for the rest, each with the record's value, the customer's best quote, its turn id and the paragraphs it may engage. On the judge's typed misled call the 11 iteration 1 flags became 2 `refer` lines, 4 `incomplete` and 3 `understanding_gap` lines (P3a report §6); the judge's ordinary informed customer, a referral in iteration 1, is no longer one (it ends `incomplete` because the iteration 1 dialogue made no read-back of the range; with the read-back and `tenure_months` on the record it is `understanding_gap`, P3a report §3). The case file adds both readings of each figure, the models and parameters, the audio hash and per-turn latencies. A flag on `kind`, `premium`, `consent`, `bundling` or `loan_funded` is the evidence pack for the bank's reviewer: the record, the customer's words with turn ids and audio offsets, and the paragraph it may engage. A flag is not mis-selling, and it does not trigger an 85Z refund.

**The reviewer queue and the half-yearly report** (`samajh/ops`, `web/review`, over a synthetic 200-loan book, `data/loanbook/README.md`). The sampler draws a share of the sales made since the previous draw that are still within 30 days of the sale (draws do not overlap, every eligible sale has about the same chance, no attempt after day 30); the attempt log keeps every attempt; the queue holds the `refer` cases, one page each (the loan, the refer lines with their quotes, the other lines, the questions left undecided, the transcript, and the decisions recorded so far; a decision is one of a list of neutral choices with a note and the reviewer's name, and carries no interval); the report is the artifact para 85Y names: the reach funnel (sampled, attempted, answered, right person confirmed, completed within 30 days) is its denominator, the tiers of completed calls and each rule's finding are grouped as features, risks and how the product was sold, each with an interval for sampling among eligible sales only. Every referral count in the report, the queue and the case pages is given as two counts side by side, on what the customer said and on the record alone (P3c, above). Real phone sessions enter the queue and the report (`samajh.ops ingest`); the batch's other calls are simulated from the builders' assumed customer mix, so no share in it says anything about a real lender. The report's own risk note says risk coverage is thin: one question bears on risk (can the money be got back), and nothing asks about exclusions, claim conditions or what happens to the cover if the loan closes early. The user decided on 2026-11-10 not to add a risk question (§15 item 39).

**Amounts.** We use `format=transcribe`, then a fraction-aware parser (sawa / saadhe / paune / dedh / dhai; Tamil and Kannada 1.5 and 2.5). The measured weakness of Prisma v3.0 is in §9 (E3). *Design consequence, adopted (§15 item 16):* each figure turn is transcribed by both v3.0 and v2.5 and `samajh.amounts.cross_check` compares the parsed readings (`Agreed(rupees)` or `ReadBackNeeded(readings)`); a disagreement or a missing reading makes the bot ask again and then read back, and the read-back stays for every critical amount whatever the readings say. v3.0 alone is not enough: its wrong amounts come with no warning. Shapes the parser deliberately refuses (it returns no amount, which leads to a re-ask and a read-back): a number word directly before digits, clock times beside amounts, a romanised fraction word ("sava lakh"), a small count before a noun ("do kisht mein 41300", "do policy"; the verifier's recheck found the romanised form read, and the last fix round refused it: builder tests only, not re-verified), Kannada ondu kaalu and ondu mukkaalu before a unit (needs a fluent Kannada reader), "3/4 lakh" in Tamil, and a word the lexicon does not know that stands before a number and looks like a fraction or unit word. Iteration 3 changed what it reads: romanised Hindi number words 0 to 99 and the units are read ("bavan hazaar" → 52,000), with a gate that keeps everyday words (do, saath, das, tera, bara) from becoming numbers; "sorry/सॉरी" refuses a figure only when a number follows it; a bound ("X तक", "के अंदर", "up to X") is read as X and marked `bound`, accepted for cover and for the bot's read-back lines and refused for the premium, where it is never carried; "ek baar X" reads X. The verifier's recheck, in its wording: "On 8 typed romanised replies using saath, do, tera, bara or das as ordinary words, the amount was read in 8 of 8." and "On 3 typed calls, a confirmed cover read-back on the bot's real line ended `clear`." The held-out amounts result stays scored with the parser as of commit 59c12d9; it was not rescored. A "one" after a hundreds word is part of the number ("तीन सौ एक दिए" = 301); after a thousand-or-larger unit and before a verb it is ambiguous, never the smaller figure; before a noun it is a determiner. The development set's after-fix numbers are in-sample and are not reported here (`eval/amounts/REPORT.md`, `references/pilots/W1-core-report.md` §3).

**Extraction model: `openai/gpt-6-luna` through OpenRouter.** This is W5b's recommendation (`references/pilots/W5-extractor-report.md` §2.4). It replaces the earlier default, the cheap `mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct`, which `references/pilots/W2-clients-report.md` chose on a 16-utterance probe. The model returns enums, clause numbers and spans, never turn ids, product ids, rupee figures or verdicts. Code resolves them against the customer's reply, so every quote kept is a slice of the reply (frozen-set run: 0 of 802 spans not in the reply, 0 of 1,195 clause numbers out of range). The model rejects the `temperature` parameter (OpenRouter answers 404), so it is not pinned to 0 and a second uncached run could differ; the extractor also now runs its requests in parallel. `samajh.pipeline.default_llm()` leaves the parameter out. It is a reasoning model and needs room: at 800 output tokens one hard reply came back cut off and empty, so the limit is 4,000.

- **Cost.** $0.00171 per transcript on the frozen set (144 transcripts, 3,391 LLM requests, $0.24589 with nothing cached, $0.00007 per LLM request) and $0.00153 per transcript on the development set (n = 20 transcripts). That is about twice Mistral ($0.00081 on the development set) and a third of `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` ($0.00502).
- **Latency.** Per LLM request, when first made: median 1.88 s, p90 3.38 s (frozen-set run; wall time of the client call, retries included). A transcript takes 20 to 24 requests; in iteration 1 they ran one after another (about 40 s at the median, inferred from the per-request median) and P3a now runs them in parallel, so the end-to-end time of a case file is not measured. **Consequence: extraction runs after the call, on the whole transcript, and not per turn.** It is fine after a call and too slow inside one. Anything the bot must decide during the call (the one re-ask of an unclear answer, §5; the read-back) cannot wait for the extractor. The dialogue decides these turns with word lists and the turn classifier (§5).
- **Why the earlier default was rejected.** On the development set (20 hand-written calls, 5 per language, 7 of them gold-clear), Mistral raised false alarms on 3 of the 7 gold-clear calls (`google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite`: 7 of 7; `openai/gpt-6-luna` and `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`: 0 of 7). Call verdicts right: gpt-6-luna 20 of 20, Haiku 20, Mistral 17, Gemini 13. Fields right (n = 304): gpt-6-luna 304, Haiku 303, Mistral 298, Gemini 285. The weaker models read a plain "I signed" or "I gave the OTP" as a sale concern. They also misread negations. On a probe of 24 Hindi and Hinglish replies written so that the first clause, read alone, gives the opposite answer, gpt-6-luna and Haiku read 24 of 24 correctly, Mistral 20 and Gemini 18. Two of Mistral's four misses were in the false-clear direction: a reply that begins "I agreed" and then denies it was read as consent given. The probe shows a failure pattern and is not a rate, and it has no Tamil or Kannada. A difference of one or two on these sets is noise (Mistral gave 18 of 20 verdicts in one run and 17 in another with the same prompts); the gap between the two groups of models is not. The development set is in-sample for the prompt, which was iterated on it, and the model was not selected on the frozen set (it is kept for one final measurement).
- **Alternative.** `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` matches gpt-6-luna on the probe, is one field behind on the development set, costs 3.3 times as much per transcript and is faster per request (median 1.04 s).
- **Not checked.** Any other caller that sends `temperature` to this model would get a 404. The dialogue's LLM fallback does not use this model by default (it names `mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct` in code, chosen on the dialogue's turn set, and sends 512 output tokens after a 30-token limit left a reasoning model's reply empty); the `--llm-model` option can name another.

## 8. Engineering constraints

- **Concurrency is 1 per account, not per key.** A second simultaneous REST request gets an instant 429 "Only 1 active request is allowed at a time", and with two keys from the same account the second still got it (6 of 6 trials, order shuffled, 2026-11-10). One queue for all requests, exponential backoff, no parallel REST calls; STT and TTS share the slot. In the probe a WebSocket streaming session did not occupy it, but streaming is not shipped (§15 item 12). A live turn waits 5 s for the key lock and then fails with `gnani_busy`, so nothing else may use the account during a call (§13). Fixed bot lines are cache hits; a read-back carries the customer's figure and is synthesised on the turn (about 1.4 s in the P1 runs, n = 2).
- **Caching:** key = sha256(model, params, audio/text). Noise and 8 kHz variants are made offline and only STT is re-run per condition.
- **Cost:** client estimates, not dashboard readings: 0.010 credits per STT audio second and 0.002 per TTS character. 325.60 of the 1,000-credit project cap are used by the client's estimate (2026-11-13; 14.80 of them spent on 2026-11-13: 9.82 by the iteration 3 call package, 10 + 6 new bot-line clips and 4 Batch jobs, `P8-report.md`, and 4.99 by a P5 voice smoke run (`eval/p5/runs/voice-smoke*`, uncommitted, not a result)); the round 3 re-voice of the fixed lines (70 per language, 92 new clips) cost about 12.6, the held-out amounts run 136.5 (`uv run python -m samajh.clients.gnani ledger` reads the local ledger). The dashboard has not been read.
- **Latency** (P1, `P1-report.md` §2; Timbre customer voices, key lightly used; n = 31 uncached turns of replayed calls, plus 4 turns by microphone in headless Chrome): STT for both readings median 364 ms (p90 569, max 1,632); the dialogue on word lists alone median 1 ms; with the LLM classifier consulted median 1,398 ms (p90 1,753; 15 of 31 turns); fixed lines from cache 3 ms; a read-back made on the turn about 1.4 s (n = 2); in Chrome, from the end of speech to the bot's audio, a median 1,145 ms (n = 4, yes/no turns only). The phone does not answer before the grace window passes (yes/no 1,000 ms to open 1,800 ms). **By microphone, one speaker:** two practice calls by the user on 2026-11-10 (20 turns: 6 yes/no, 2 short, 1 amount, 11 open; `P1b-ownvoice-timing.md`, not independently verified) had a modelled median of 3,441 ms from the end of speech to the reply audio (p90 5,474, max 6,665; modelled, not logged). The main cause was lines missing from the cache: 22 of 35 bot lines were made during the calls (median 1,043 ms each). The classifier added a median of about 1.1 s on the open turns where it was consulted (inferred from times over 300 ms). The fixed lines have been re-voiced since (P1c), so this is not the phone's present speed, and one speaker says nothing about anyone else.
- **End of speech** (`web/phone.js`, `samajh/server/settings.toml`): the cut and the grace come from the kind of reply the last bot line calls for (yes/no 700/1,000 ms, short 800/1,200, amount 1,000/1,600, open 1,100/1,800). At the cut the take is sent; if the customer resumes inside the grace window the phone sends the whole answer as part 2, which supersedes part 1. In headless Chrome a 1,300 ms pause on the yes/no question lost the second half (only "हाँ जी।" was heard). None of these values was set on human audio. In the two practice calls no turn was cut early; the longest pause inside an answer was 620 ms, in a birth-year answer against its 800 ms cut, and on one 4.5 s open answer the detector's noise estimate had risen, so the cut may have come while the user was still sounding (whether words were lost cannot be told). Pause statistics are logged per turn so the values can be set once more human audio exists.
- **Backend proxy** (FastAPI, `samajh/server/`): browsers can't set `X-API-Key-ID`. The Gnani and OpenRouter keys live in environment variables only. The server resamples uploads to the line rate with ffmpeg, binds 127.0.0.1, refuses other Host and Origin values and has no login. A call's log and dialogue state are one atomic file, so a call resumes after a restart. After the call a background job builds the case file; a failed step is named and the case is `failed`, never given a tier.
- **LLM hosting:** v1 sends only synthetic transcripts to OpenRouter. A real lender must check where transcripts may go first: the RBI Digital Lending Directions, 2025, para 13(iv) (read in the saved text of the Directions; noted under ledger row E2.9), say data is to be stored only on servers in India and that data processed outside India is to be deleted there and brought back to India within 24 hours of processing. Whether this reaches a given lender's call transcripts is unchecked.
- **Synthetic data only:** obviously fake names, loan IDs, years of birth and numbers. Ombudsman cases are re-created with invented parties, never quoted with real names.

**Gold standard and circularity.** 11 of the 36 base cases are templated on public awards: five of the customer-relief awards in §1, BNG-L-033-1617-0600 (a policy bought "as a requirement of a security deposit for the overdraft facility"), BHP-L-013-1617-0436 (an "Fixed Deposit to get the housing loan"), Baramulla consumer commission CC 33/2025 v J&K Bank (₹2,00,000 debited towards an insurance policy, with no material found that it was done with consent; the bank did not appear and was proceeded against ex parte; our copy of that order is a scan hosted by a news site), and three awards named nowhere else here: BHP-L-021-1617-0456, PUN-L-041-1617-0649 and PUN-L-009-1617-0009. Compliant scenarios are written from the rules; awards the insurer won are not used as compliant gold. Gold verdicts are written first and **frozen with a hash before any audio is made**. Each templated case records the remedy in its award: 5 refund, 4 conversion, 1 partial refund, 1 other; of the 22 relief awards we read, about two-thirds ordered a refund and one-third a conversion. The set is built to exercise each rule, not to mirror how often a problem occurs, so no rate on it says anything about prevalence. Speech is templated, one to three variants per answer. The frozen set is for final measurement only: never tune on it. Datasheet and history: `data/scenarios/README.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`.

**What teach-back is, and what we don't claim for it.** Teach-back means asking a person to explain in their own words what they were told. It is well studied in healthcare; the newest systematic review (2026) found no clear pattern of effect on knowledge. We found no study of teach-back for loan or insurance sales, or by an automated voice. Most of this rests on abstracts, not full papers (C3).

## 12. Why we think this is not yet common (our read)

1. **The rule is new.** It was issued on 15 June 2026 and takes effect on 1 January 2027.
2. **A low-cost survey is not excluded.** 85Y names surveys as an example and sets no channel, so a low-cost survey is not excluded by the text. A low-cost survey is the likely default. That is our inference. We found no bank or industry statement on how banks will comply.
3. **Most voice-AI vendors lead with support, sales and collections.** Of 11 vendor sites checked on 10 November 2026, 10 list support, 9 sales and 8 collections. Seven also sell audit or compliance products, and some market adjacent post-sale checks (§11). None of those pages cites 85Y.
4. **It's harder than it looks (our view).** It needs product records, deterministic comparison, and correct Indian amounts. In our 20-sentence TTS pilot (one voice), Prisma's digit output ignored the fraction word in 8 transcripts across 5 sentences. In the 240-sentence development set (TTS audio, simulated phone line; `eval/amounts/REPORT.md` §2.7) reading Prisma's digits naively was right on 9 of 60 fraction sentences on v2.5 (15%, 8-26) and 54 of 60 on v3.0 (90%, 80-95). It is a measure of TTS audio, not of real callers.

What Samajh adds to the adjacent products: a deterministic comparison with the lender's record, an evidence log with quotes and audio offsets, and a measured evaluation. The open question before any product is named is ours too, but it is not a claim of novelty: Edesy's blog ("confirm what the customer understood") and a hackathon entry, Saakshi ("three to five teach-back questions"), already describe teach-back-style questions (ledger C2.3, C2.5), and SquadStack's page describes a call that asks whether the borrower understands (C2.2). Lead with the first three.

Unknowns: banks may build it in-house or adapt survey bots, and we found no public statement from any bank on how it will run 85Y feedback. No regulator or survey we found reports how many loans carry bundled insurance. The numbers that exist count complaints.
