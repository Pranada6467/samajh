# CLAUDE.md

## What this project is
Preparation and build workspace for Gnani AI's **Great Indian AI Internship Challenge** — a student challenge to build a working application on Gnani AI's Indian-language voice models/APIs. Full research lives in `RESEARCH.md`; read it before proposing ideas or architecture.

## The goal
- Build a **genuinely working, measured** voice application on at least one Gnani model or API.
- The user prioritises **build quality** over social engagement, and wants to **learn** while building.
- Target one or two specific award categories (see `RESEARCH.md` §1); a team can win only one award.
- Show understanding of the real Indian voice-AI market (Gnani sells mostly to BFSI, lenders, telecom, insurance, contact centres) — this matters for the interview stage.

## Submission requirements (for reference)
- Working app shown in a public demo video (a strong 60-second cut is valuable).
- Video posted on LinkedIn and/or X tagging Gnani AI with `#GnaniAI` and `#GreatIndianAIInternshipChallenge`.
- Written explanation: title, problem, target users, Gnani models/APIs used (name exact versions), each member's contribution.

## Hard constraints
- **Synthetic data only.** Never use real phone numbers, account numbers, Aadhaar, PAN, or recordings of real calls/people. Generated IDs must obey formats but be obviously fake. Self-recorded clips from consenting teammates are fine.
- **Agent Builder is out of scope.** Use the Speech APIs (STT, TTS, voice cloning) directly.
- Evon LLM is not on the API — use any LLM for reasoning, or self-host Evon.

## Gnani API facts (verified live on 2026-11-10; evidence in `references/pilots/api-probe-iter1.md`)
Where docs.gnani.ai and live behaviour differ, live wins. `[docs]` marks what was read in the docs and not exercised: re-verify it before relying on it.
- Base URL `https://api.vachana.ai`; auth header `X-API-Key-ID` (one key serves STT, TTS and Batch) → browsers can't set it on WebSockets, so use a backend proxy.
- **Prisma STT REST:** `POST /stt/v3` serves both models; the `model` field picks `prisma-v2.5` (the default if omitted) or `prisma-v3.0`. `language_code` is required. Always send `format`: the server's default changes with model and language. Clips ≤30 s inclusive on both models (31 s gives 400 `MAX_AUDIO_DURATION_EXCEEDED`).
- **v2.5:** `format` is `verbatim` or `transcribe`; no `indic-auto`. `format=transcribe` writes numbers as digits for Tamil and Kannada as well as Hindi and English (the docs say Hindi/English only), and the reply's `output.literal` holds the wording before formatting. `output.literal` equals the verbatim wording of the same clip, ignoring punctuation and spaces: 112 of 112 phone-line clips (eval/amounts/REPORT.md, TTS audio). v2.5 answered 504 on 15 of 1,184 calls (1.3%; 14 of the 15 Tamil or Kannada), each succeeding on a second attempt after a wait of about 30 s, inferred from the ledger times with the `Retry-After` header not read; v3.0 did so once in 1,456 calls (eval/amounts/REPORT.md, TTS audio). `bias_list` ≤100 letter-only words `[docs]` is accepted, effect not measured; the substitution map (≤10 `[docs]`) is accepted, effect not shown.
- **v3.0:** `indic-auto` and comma-separated language lists work on REST only (the streaming endpoint rejects the list; see the next bullet). `format` is `verbatim`, `transcribe`, `transliterate` or `codemix` (`codemix` is a `format` value, not a separate option, and v2.5 rejects it). `keyterms` (≤200 `[docs]`) changed the output; `topic` takes free text. `format=transcribe` writes some amounts that skip a place-value group as a different amount ("three lakh two hundred" as 3,02,000), confidently and with the words gone, so only a read-back or a second reading catches them: see the next bullet.
- **Held-out result on amounts that skip a place-value group** (`eval/amounts_v2/`; `references/reviews/iter2/verify-P6.md` reproduced every number; first seen on the development set in `eval/amounts/REPORT.md`). Set: 280 Timbre-voiced Hindi and Hinglish sentences, 68 skipped-group amounts each with a matched control (same carrier, voice and leading group), frozen before any audio. **Matched comparison:** on simulated 8 kHz phone audio Prisma v3.0 `transcribe` wrote a different amount for 40 of 68 skipped-group amounts (59%, 95% CI 47-70) and for 0 of 68 matched controls (exact McNemar p = 1.8e-12). Its `verbatim` output of the same audio had the right number words in all 40, and v2.5 `transcribe` wrote none wrong. **Reported apart:** 14 of the 40 are an "X lakh Y" phrase with Y under 100 written as X lakh Y thousand ("तीन लाख चालीस" as 3,40,000), which colloquial Hindi may mean as 3.4 lakh, so whether they count is an open question for a native speaker; the other 26 are wrong under any reading ("one lakh three hundred" as 1,300; "five crore twenty-six thousand" as 5,26,000). **Agreement rule:** accepting an amount only when v3.0 and v2.5 `transcribe` agree accepted 224 of 280 synthetic clips (80%) with no wrong amount accepted. **Real voices, separate:** the same rule accepted 113 of 124 public Hindi recordings (91%; IndicVoices and Kathbath, read and extempore speech) with no wrong amount accepted; 107 of the 124 are from the datasets' train splits, which Gnani may have trained on, so this may be optimistic, and only 4 recordings skip a group (v3.0 wrong on 1: too few for a rate). **Not claimed:** the pooled 14% (the skipped stratum is 24% of the set by design; 6 of 1,358 public utterances skip a group), any per-call or retail-band rate, "proven formatting bug" (the measured fact is that the verbatim words are right; the cause is an inference), "fails whenever an amount skips a group" (23 of the 68 were right), "tested on real callers", and "validated" for the cheaper variant that compares v3.0 with v2.5's `output.literal` (chosen on these same two sets).
- **Streaming and Batch:** WebSocket streaming (`/stt/v3/stream`) was measured against REST per turn and is not built into the server; the decision and numbers are in `references/pilots/voice-loop-iter1.md` §1 and §3. On 20 TTS clips (TTS clips only, no human speech) streaming at the server's default 500 ms silence returned the transcript a median 0.78 s after the end of speech, against about 0.71 s for a browser detector at 500 ms plus a REST call (n = 20 each); the default cut all 4 clips with a 600 or 900 ms pause into two segments, and streaming billing is unknown. **Streaming rejects a comma-separated `language_code` on v3.0** (`hi-IN,en-IN` gives "exactly one locale is required", then close 1008; 13 of 13 attempts), contrary to the docs, which say 1 to 4 codes; REST accepts the list. Prisma Batch works end to end (create, start, poll, fetch the transcript from a pre-signed URL) and returns segment start/end times and speaker ids. Segments follow pauses and speaker changes, not sentences: two similar voices joined with no pause came back as one segment (n = 2 TTS clips; nothing yet on real phone audio). Denoising and long-file limits are `[docs]`.
- **Timbre TTS:** `POST /api/v1/tts/inference` works on v2.5 and v3.0 (13 voices on v3.0, 73 on v2.5; no list-voices endpoint). The server doesn't validate `language`. Every WAV has streaming-style size fields that break Python's `wave`; the client repairs them. SSE, WebSocket, number/date/ID normalisation and audio tags (v3.0) are `[docs]`. **Voice cloning** (embeddings + REST/SSE/WebSocket) is `[docs]`.
- No confidence scores (Batch segments carry `confidence: null`) and no word timestamps (Batch gives segment times; REST gives none) — derive confidence from validation and read-back.
- **Concurrency is 1 per account, not per key.** A second simultaneous REST request gets an instant 429 `RATE_LIMITED` (0.2 to 0.3 s, "Only 1 active request is allowed at a time") with no `Retry-After`: it is rejected, not queued, and STT and TTS share the slot. With two keys from the same account, one request on each at the same moment, exactly one succeeded and the other got the 429, in 6 of 6 trials with the order shuffled (2026-11-10); a second key (`GNANI_API_KEY_LIVE` in `.env`) is a spare only. A WebSocket streaming session does not occupy the slot. Batch routes have a separate 429 for calls less than about 1 s apart. Not established: whether a running Batch job holds the slot. Use the single-flight lock, exponential backoff, and a cache of every response.
- **Credits:** 5,000 on the account; the project caps itself at 1,000 and has used about 295 by estimate (2026-11-10; no dashboard reading yet). No API surface reports cost (no header, no body field, no usage endpoint); the dashboard at app.gnani.ai is the only source. The client's guard runs on estimates: 0.010 credits per STT audio second and 0.002 per TTS character (`samajh/clients/defaults.toml`), rounded up from one pilot dashboard reading (about 0.0087 and 0.0018, read off a chart, so each could be 20% off or more). Batch is assumed to bill like STT; streaming billing is unknown.
- Unsupported languages `[docs]`: Odia, Assamese, Bhojpuri. Live, v3.0 rejects Assamese (400 `MODEL_UNSUPPORTED_LANGUAGE`), matching the docs; the others were not exercised.
- The challenge page names v2.5 models; v3.0 is newer. Build so either works, and report results for both.

## How to work in this repo
- Prefer the converged design pattern: capture speech → LLM extracts into a strict schema → **deterministic** validation against the official rule/form (no LLM arithmetic) → TTS read-back of critical fields → evidence log → evaluation on a synthetic, rule-derived test set.
- Every accuracy claim needs a metric and a gold standard. Report failure cases honestly; include self-recorded audio, not only TTS audio.
- Ground regulatory claims in primary sources and cite them; flag anything unverified.
- Regulatory and case claims must match `references/claims/claims-ledger.md` (its allowed and banned wording).
- The extraction LLM sees no record values for the fields under test and no borrower name (the right-party and second identity checks are never sent to it), and every quote must be a verbatim whole clause of the customer's turn, never a fragment that stops short of a later "not". Code, not the model, resolves which turn, which product and which clause; a quote not found in the reply becomes `Unclear(quote_not_grounded)`. The only record value a lexical check found in the bot questions shown to the model is the premium in a read-back question, which repeats the customer's own amount and so equals the record's premium whenever the customer's amount does; the check cannot see a kind or payment source put into a question in words.
- Update documentation as soon as a verified result makes it stale.
- `data/amounts/amounts-v1.jsonl` is a development set for the amount parser: scores on it after the parser fixes are in-sample. `data/amounts_v2/amounts-v2.jsonl` is the held-out set (new seed, frozen before any audio, scored with the parser as of commit 59c12d9). Do not tune the parser on its failures; a later headline amount number needs another new frozen set. All TTS audio: no amount figure from it is a claim about real callers. Real-voice amounts are a separate result with their own caveats (above).
- **Models:** Fable only orchestrates (plans, briefs, decides); Opus medium builds; a separate Opus high that wrote none of the code verifies, on inputs the builder never saw; Sonnet does research and documentation. Subagents cannot write new report files: they return the report as text and the coordinator saves it. Details: `ORCHESTRATION.md`.
- **The outcome is a four-tier triage:** `refer` (a human must look), `incomplete` (the call did not yield enough to decide), `understanding_gap` (reported in aggregate, not queued), `clear`. Silence is never a pass. Report misses and referrals as counts per 100 with the n and by kind of customer, never as one pooled rate. A flag is evidence for review, never a finding.
- **Hindi and Hinglish are the product languages.** Tamil and Kannada stay in the code as experimental: appendix only, labelled `not human-reviewed`, no new effort, not in the demo or any headline.
- **E1 (teach-back against a scripted yes/no call) is not a headline:** it is true by construction. The replacement is package P5, customers the builders did not script with a matched closed arm. A builder's own probe sets are not evidence of a fix; an independent verifier's inputs are.
- Do not claim "nobody has built this" or "no vendor offers this": adjacent vendors exist (SquadStack, Edesy, Ringg, NuSummit). Say what Samajh adds: an open question before any product is named, a deterministic comparison with the lender's record, an evidence log, a measured evaluation.
- A flag produces an evidence pack for human review, never a "refund case" and never a mis-selling finding: the 85Z refund duty arises only where mis-selling is established, and a flag is not that.
- Avoid saturated themes unless there's a clear, evidenced twist: kirana billing, govt-scheme helpers, generic ASHA intake, scam-call shields, farmer helplines, tutors, interview coaches.
- The spoken-numbers/FASTag direction was explored and dropped (weak demand evidence) — don't revive it without new evidence.
- The women's-safety distress agent ("Pukaar") was explored and dropped (evidence/product mismatch, bot-to-112 risk, concurrency limit, weak Gnani-market fit; `RESEARCH.md` §10b) — don't revive it without new evidence.
- For real-voice STT evaluation, use public consent-based datasets per `references/real-voice-datasets.md` — evaluation only, never in the demo or repo.

- `samajh/types.py`, `samajh/amounts/`, `samajh/rules/` — the core. Triage types: `Tier` (`refer`, `incomplete`, `understanding_gap`, `clear`), `tier_of`, `FlagReason` and `FLAG_TIER` (the one table that gives each flag its tier), `Gap`/`GapReason`, `Stated` (with `hedged`, `approximate`, `low`, `also`), `AnswerConflict`, `PaymentFrequency`. `ProductResult.verdict` and `CaseResult.verdict` stay because the frozen gold is written in them; new code reads `tier`. `samajh.rules.summarise(record, result) -> CaseSummary` gives a reviewer three lines instead of every flag. `samajh.amounts` has `parse_amount`, `parse_approx_amount` (a hedge or a narrow range becomes `Approximate`), `cross_check` and `MAX_RANGE_RATIO`. - `samajh/extract/`, `samajh/pipeline.py` — the LLM extractor: `extract(transcript, record, llm) -> RawExtraction`. The model gets one question and one reply per request (requests run in parallel) and returns enums, clause numbers and spans only; code grounds every quote against the reply and never trims one. A failed model call makes only its own fields `Unclear(extraction_failed)`. `run_text_case(transcript, record) -> CaseResult`; `default_llm()` builds the OpenRouter client and leaves out `temperature` for a model that rejects it, as `openai/gpt-6-luna` (the extraction model, `DESIGN.md` §7) does. Tests: `tests/extract/`. Evidence: `references/pilots/W5-extractor-report.md`, `P3a-report.md`.
- `eval/text_path/` — the text-path evaluation: `devset.py` (20 hand-written calls, in-sample for the prompt), `run.py`, `scoring.py` (triage by kind of customer; E1 is kept only for history), `negation_probe.py` and `runs/` (the reports, including `frozen__openai-gpt-6-luna.md`; iteration 1's are in `runs/iter1/`). Results are on scripted text, not speech: an upper bound for the voice path. The frozen set was run once in iteration 1 and rerun from the cache after P3a's changes; never tune on it.
- `samajh/dialogue/` — **fix round in progress (package P2; a builder is editing it, so read it as a moving target).** The call as a pure state machine: `start(record, language, lender_name=...)` and `step(state, customer_text, second_reading=...)` (`machine.py`), a rule interpreter over word lists (`interpret.py`, `_lexicon.py`), an LLM turn classifier used beside the word lists (`llm_fallback.py`, a 2.5 s wall-clock limit; on a failure the turn keeps the word lists' reading and is marked degraded; the fix round makes it the primary judge at the opening), the bot's line keys (`prompts.py`) and the banned-wording lint (`lint.py`). Eleven outcomes, among them `call_back_later`, `language_switch` and `no_response`. Play a call: `uv run python -m samajh.dialogue play --record tests/dialogue/fixtures/record_gold_loan.json --lang hi` (`hi`, `hi-en`, `ta`, `kn`). Tests: `tests/dialogue/` (923 collected before the fix round). Contract: `references/pilots/P2-contract.md` section 5. **Independent verification found it not ready for a live line** (third parties and refusals; `DESIGN.md` §13). Results are on synthetic text, not speech; no fluent human has read any language's lines; Tamil and Kannada are not human-reviewed.
- `data/prompts/` — the bot's lines: `hi.toml`, `hi-en.toml`, `ta.toml`, `kn.toml` (63 line templates each at P2's build; the fix round may change this), every line naming the DESIGN.md paragraph it implements. Templates only; the slots are the first name, the lender name, an ordinal and (read-backs) the figure the customer said. **Rule: no bot line may reveal a record value under test (kind, premium, payment source, cover, term, cancel window, consent, provider choice) or ask for an OTP, PIN, password, account number or date of birth.** The lines that say the customer will never be asked for those (`disclosure.never_ask`, `verify.*`) are the only ones that name them. The second check asks for the year of birth only. No line may hold a digit, name the product (insurance, policy, premium, cover, FD, deposit), suggest how it was paid for or how consent was given, state a right or promise money back. `load_prompts` refuses a file that fails the lint, but the lint is a word list, so a person must read any change to these files. A changed line needs the tests rerun and the voiced lines rebuilt; the Tamil and Kannada files keep the label `not human-reviewed`.
