# Verification of P6 (harden the Gnani amounts finding)

Verifier: independent, no Gnani calls, no edits outside this file and `probes/`. Date 2026-11-10.
Probes (offline, rerunnable): `probes/p6_recompute.py tts|realvoice` (exports `samajh/amounts` **and** `samajh/types.py` at 59c12d9 under new names, reads `stt.jsonl` directly, own Wilson and exact McNemar), `p6_ledger.py`, `p6_heldout.py`, `p6_skipped_detail.py`, `p6_leakcheck.py`.

**Verdict: the numbers reproduce exactly, and the held-out freeze holds. The core bug is real. The headline framing needs four corrections before it goes public: a rate that the design sets, an example that colloquial usage makes ambiguous, a recommendation selected in-sample, and a "shape and nothing else" claim.**

## Findings, by severity

1. **High (wording): 14% and 11% are rates the design sets, not field rates.** The skipped stratum is 68 of 280 items (24%) by construction. In the real-voice candidate pool, 6 of 1,358 utterances (0.4%) skip a group. "Returned a wrong amount on 40 (14%)" in both drafts invites a reading as how often v3.0 gets amounts wrong. Report the stratum rate (40/68) and the control (0/68). Drop the pooled rates from headlines.
2. **Medium-high: 14 of the 40 failures (12 of the 23 in the retail band) follow one pattern.** They are "X lakh Y" with Y < 100, written as X lakh Y thousand: "तीन लाख चालीस" becomes 3,40,000, and "two lakh fourteen" becomes 2,14,000. In colloquial Indian speech "teen lakh chalis" is commonly said for 3.4 lakh (my judgement; a native speaker should confirm). For that pattern the spoken words are not unambiguous to a human listener, and Gnani may call the output a defensible reading. The bug report leads with exactly these examples. The other 26 failures are unambiguous: the lakh dropped ("one lakh three hundred" → 1,300), the crore dropped ("पाँच करोड़ छब्बीस हज़ार" → 5,26,000), the final group dropped ("दो हज़ार बारह" → 2,000), or hundreds turned into thousands ("four lakh nine hundred" → 4,09,000). Lead with those. Naturalness: the carrier sentences are natural. Many skipped amounts are not amounts a borrower would say (a premium of Rs 3,00,040, an instalment of "two crore seventy thousand"). The 12 crore+thousand failures all fall outside the retail band. "Silent wrong" is fairly defined, and conservative toward v3.0: its 5 malformed outputs (`12,81`) count as refusals.
3. **Medium: the recommendation was selected in-sample.** experiment.toml fixed the reference scheme and the decision rule before the audio. The candidate schemes (SCHEMES in analyze.py, readings.py) were first committed at 13:52 IST, after the TTS run. T3 = L25 was then picked by coverage on the same two sets that report its 0 errors. Among the four two-request schemes, it and the reference are the only ones with 0 wrong on both sets. The 95% upper bounds for wrong-accepted are about 1.6% (0/234) and 3.2% (0/117). The test is a fresh frozen TTS set with a new seed, plus new real clips (dataset valid or test splits, self-recorded teammates), evaluated once. There is also one known path to a correlated error: the 59c12d9 parser reads "एक लाख एक" as 1,00,000, which is also the shape of v3.0's drop-the-final-group error. That clip was safe only because v3.0 read it right.
4. **Medium: the bug report's mechanism claims overreach.** It says "depends on the shape of the amount and nothing else". The data show otherwise: 23 of 68 skipped items were right on phone. By shape, phone wrong rates are lakh+units 16/18, lakh+hundreds 8/18, thousand+units 4/20 and crore+thousands 12/12. 7 of 68 items flip between the clean and the mu-law copy of the same clip. The transcribe output is also not a pure normalisation of the verbatim output: on one clip v3.0 added a "rupees" nobody spoke, on another it merged "पाँच आए" into "पाए", on a third it dropped a word. That suggests a separate decode, so "formatting, not recognition" is an inference. The measured fact is that the verbatim output of the same audio has the right words.
5. **Medium: the real-voice clips may overlap training data.** 108 of 125 clips come from the datasets' *train* splits (IndicVoices 73, Kathbath 35). These are public AI4Bharat corpora that an Indic ASR vendor may have trained on, so the real-voice accuracy could be optimistic. This also departs from `references/real-voice-datasets.md` (IndicVoices valid split, Kathbath noisy test). The clips are solo phone-app recordings: 90 read, 34 extempore, many of them scripted command prompts. 103 of the 124 golds are multiples of 100. Selection required the parser to read exactly one amount from the reference, which excludes the harder phrasings. It did, however, put multi-group amounts first (36% under Rs 1,000 against 52% in the pool). The accuracy does not rest on trivial numbers: 72/72 for gold ≥ 1,000. But only 15 clips are ≥ 1,000 and not a multiple of 1,000.
6. **Low.** Wilson and McNemar treat the clips as independent, while the set has 13 voices and 24 carriers, and clean and phone are the same clips, not a replication. With 40 against 0 the conclusion survives. The pairing is genuine: 68 of 68 pairs share carrier, voice and leading group, so McNemar is the right test. With 0 control failures, though, the p-value adds nothing beyond "large effect" (an unpaired Fisher test gives 4e-16).
7. **Low.** The repro script costs about 2.1 credits by the client's own estimates (10 TTS clips plus 30 STT requests), against the "about 1.5" in the report. Otherwise the script matches the report. It reads the key from `GNANI_API_KEY` only and never prints it. It sends the same multipart field (`audio_file`), TTS body and voices as the run, and prints the spoken amount, each transcript and ok/WRONG. All 8 skipped sentences were wrong and both controls right, in both conditions, in the cached run. It was not run live, by the project's rule.
8. **Info: held-out integrity.** Against v1 there are 0 shared sentences and 0 shared carrier skeletons. 13 items share an amount, all fractions, and are tagged `v1_amount`. The number wording, however, comes from the same `spoken_amount` generator as v1, and the 59c12d9 parser was fixed on Prisma's outputs of that phrasing. So the set is held out for amounts and carriers, not for phrasing. For the Prisma claim itself this is a true out-of-sample replication of a hypothesis formed on v1. Git does not independently prove the freeze, because the set was first committed after the run; the filesystem times and the ledger do. Hygiene: no dataset text or audio is in tracked or unignored files. The leak scan's only hit was the generic phrase "हज़ार नौ सौ रुपये का" in v1 synthetic data. The 34 untracked fixture WAVs belong to another package and match no real-voice hash. Neither draft holds real-voice text beyond short number phrases.

## Wording for a public submission

**Allow:** "On a held-out synthetic set built to stress amounts that skip a place-value group, Prisma v3.0 `transcribe` wrote a different amount for 40 of 68 such amounts (59%, 95% CI 47-70) on simulated 8 kHz phone audio, and for 0 of 68 matched controls. Its `verbatim` output of the same audio had the right number words in all 40, and v2.5 wrote none wrong." "Examples: 'one lakh three hundred' → 1,300; 'five crore twenty-six thousand' → 5,26,000." "Accepting an amount only when v3.0 and v2.5 agree accepted 224 of 280 synthetic clips and 113 of 124 public read or extempore Hindi recordings, with no wrong amount accepted." "In public recordings the pattern appeared in 1 of 4 skipped-group amounts: too few for a rate."

**Forbid:** "v3.0 gets 14% (or 1 in 7) of amounts wrong"; "11% of retail premiums"; any per-call or field rate. "100% accurate", "zero errors" or "guarantees" for any scheme without its n and set. "Proven formatting bug" or "a separate formatter stage". "Fails whenever an amount skips a group" or "depends only on shape". "Tested on real calls, callers or borrowers". "Validated" for the T3 = L25 rule; say "chosen on these two sets, not yet tested on fresh data". "Three lakh forty → 3,40,000" as an unqualified headline error.
