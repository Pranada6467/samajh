What this report is and is not

Synthetic batch: the loan book and the customers are synthetic. Calls marked Simulated were scripted; calls marked Phone call were made on the browser phone with synthetic records.

  • The loan book and the customers on it are synthetic. Every phone call was made against a synthetic record, so no real lender and no customer of a lender is behind any number here.
  • 112 attempt(s) in this batch were simulated: their outcome and any customer reply were scripted from phrase banks, as text only, with no speech recorded or transcribed. 11 call(s) were phone sessions on the browser phone (1 attempt(s) in the sample and 10 call(s) outside it): their customer turns were read by Gnani Prisma speech-to-text (each case file names the model) from the customer audio the next note counts.
  • Calls marked Phone call were made on the browser phone against synthetic records: 11 phone session(s), 0 with customer audio from the microphone only, 4 from prepared audio files only, 0 from both, 0 with no customer audio, 7 where it is not recorded, or not readable, in the case file. None of them is a call with a real customer of a lender.
  • The share of calls flagged under each rule follows from the mix of customers the simulation assumes and, for phone calls, from what was said on the browser phone against synthetic records. It says nothing about any real lender.
  • Each interval is a 95% Wilson interval for sampling among eligible sales only (sales a draw covered). It does not cover sales no draw covered, errors in reading the call, or customers who were not reached.
  • The flag counts describe customers who completed the call. Customers who did not answer, declined or ended the call early may differ.
  • A referral is a request for a person to look at one call, and nothing more.
  • Reviewer decisions are counted, not estimated: they are one reviewer's view of the file, and no interval is given for them.

Synthetic date: the report is dated by the simulated book's clock, the day of the last call attempt.

Sampling

200 sales were made in the period. 200 were eligible (a draw covered them) and 80 were sampled. 0 were never eligible.

Each draw took 40% of the sales made since the previous draw that were still within 30 days of the sale, so draw windows never overlap. Every eligible sale had about the same chance of being sampled: between 37.5% and 41.7% (the highest is 1.11 times the lowest, within the 1.25 this report allows). Shares and intervals describe the 200 eligible sales only. The 0 sale(s) in the period that no draw covered are not represented. Shares are unweighted: with chances this close, weighting would move a share by at most 5.6% of the difference between the groups it mixes and widen an interval by under 1%.

Flags by rule: features and risks

para 85Y asks that this feedback be used to review product features and risks. Each rule runs on every counted call. Undecided calls had a gap under the rule and no flag. Referred is the number of the flagged calls that went to a reviewer for that rule. Understanding-gap calls are counted calls whose outcome is an understanding gap, with a gap under the rule; the rule with the most is the most common gap at the top of the page.

Risk coverage is thin. One question in the call bears directly on risk: whether the customer can get the money back (any time, after a period, or with a return), which shows what they believe happens to the money. Describing the product as a deposit also bears on it. The call asks nothing about exclusions, claim conditions or what happens to the cover if the loan is closed early.

Seriousness is the builders' ranking, not a regulatory scale: a belief about what the product is, whether it returns money, or how it was agreed to ranks above forgetting a figure.
