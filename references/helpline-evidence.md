# Helpline reliability in India — verified evidence

> Gathered for the dropped Pukaar idea (RESEARCH.md §10b). The facts stay valid; reuse them for anything touching emergency response or call follow-up. Note: these audits show the *state* failing to call the caller back — they are not evidence about family/friends responding.

Checked 2026-11-10 by an independent research pass. Extracted CAG/MHA text is in `helpline-sources/` (`punjab.txt`, `mp_exec.txt`, `mp_ch3.txt`, `kar.txt`, `pcr.txt`, `ners.txt`) for re-checking quotes.

## Bottom line
There is **no published national or state figure** for how many 112 or 181 calls go unanswered or how fast they're picked up. MHA set the targets in 2015; nobody reports against them. The hard evidence is state CAG audits (2014–2024). Where measured, **calls ringing out was a small problem (2.9–5.5% abandoned); the big failure was what happens after: almost no call-backs on dropped calls, and slow forwarding and dispatch.** The national average time to reach a caller is improving.

## Counter-evidence (cite it — it makes the pitch credible)
- National response times are falling; Chandigarh 5.6 min, UP 6.83 min, Himachal ~4 min, Haryana 7 min (state self-reports, 2025–26).
- Delhi High Court's 2020 test of 112 got a quick answer and a call-back.
- Measured abandon rates are low (2.9% Karnataka 108; 5.5% Delhi 100).

## Don't use (outdated or unsourced)
- "Haryana police responded to 5.2% of 181 calls" — politician quoting unnamed reports; no data source.
- Bengaluru "7.65% wrong calls" — no source found.
- Maharashtra 181 "10am–6pm" (2015), Delhi 181 "3 lines, 16 staff" (2014, not 2012), Delhi HC "disappointing" (2015), Tamil Nadu 181 dropped calls (2019), Quint "3 calls no answer" (2019, a noise complaint) — all outdated or anecdotal.

## Gaps
No 112/181 pick-up time, abandon rate or call-back data after 2019 anywhere. No post-2019 adoption data for 112 India app, Himmat Plus, Kavalan. No panic-button compliance testing.
